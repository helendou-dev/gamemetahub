After 20 years of dormancy, Capcom's samurai action series is back. **Onimusha: Way of the Sword** saw its review embargo lift on August 31 to a broadly enthusiastic reception: **86 on Metacritic** ([48 critic reviews](https://www.metacritic.com/game/onimusha-way-of-the-sword/)), **85 on OpenCritic** ([63 outlets](https://opencritic.com/game/20764/onimusha-way-of-the-sword)), and the franchise's first-ever perfect score from IGN. Built on RE Engine and directed by Motohide Esashi, the game arrives **Thursday, September 4** on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC — moved up three weeks from its original September 25 date.

Capcom has already delivered three acclaimed releases in 2026 — Resident Evil Requiem (89), Pragmata (85), and Monster Hunter Stories 3 — and Onimusha makes it four. It also swept Gamescom 2026 with **Best Gameplay** and **Best PlayStation Game**. But the scores tell a more nuanced story than the aggregate suggests: critics are nearly unanimous on the combat, yet deeply divided on whether the 25-hour campaign earns its length.

## What Critics Agree On

The consensus is remarkably strong on three points. First, the **Issen counter system** — perfectly timed strikes that can one-shot enemies — earned near-universal praise. IGN's Will Borger wrote that the game leaves room for you to "truly understand its action and overcome their absence through sheer determination," calling it a testament to the combat that he beat the game without using assist systems. Second, **Miyamoto Musashi**: Capcom modeled the protagonist on the likeness of legendary actor Toshiro Mifune, and even critical reviews praised the characterization — Borger noted Capcom captured Mifune's "habit of scratching the back of his head and the way he walks." Third, the **boss fights**: encounters against historical rivals like Sasaki Ganryu and Benkei were singled out across positive and mixed reviews alike as the game's creative high points.

The RE Engine visuals also drew consistent praise, with Hey Poor Player calling the game "visually impressive" and Nintendo Life noting it "takes its gameplay down a new and exciting path without losing its essential identity."

## What Splits Critics

The fault lines are clear and consistent across the lower-scoring reviews:

- **Campaign length vs. content.** Polygon called the game "an excellent 12-hour action game stretched into a 25-hour slog." Game Informer echoed this, noting the game "struggles to justify the repetition" of fighting the same mini-bosses multiple times with "no discernible reason beyond those darned demons doing silly little demon things."
- **Open world vs. linear design.** Kotaku's review was the harshest take, arguing Capcom "removed too much of the series' identity" by adding an open world with side missions and collectables instead of the interconnected spaces of older entries. The result, they wrote, is "a game at war with itself."
- **RPG progression.** Multiple reviewers found the upgrade systems — several overlapping currencies feeding incremental improvements — flat compared to the depth of the combat itself. RogerEbert.com praised the "weighty" and "meticulous" feel of every fight but noted the progression never matches it.

## The Bottom Line

An 86 Metacritic with IGN's first 10/10 for the franchise says Capcom has successfully revived Onimusha for the modern era. The Issen system is the real deal — a combat mechanic that rewards precision and patience in a way few contemporary action games attempt. But this is a game that asks 25 hours of your time, and by most accounts, not all of them are earned. If you are here for the swordplay and the boss duels, buy it at launch. If you want a tight, focused action campaign, wait for community impressions from the second half. Either way, with [Nioh 3](/games/nioh-3) and [The Blood of Dawnwalker](/games/blood-of-dawnwalker) also dropping this week, September 2026 is an extraordinarily expensive month for action fans.

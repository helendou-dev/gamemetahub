The single thing that makes Resonant different from the original Control is not the melee combat or the open Manhattan. It is the Resonant boss class, and the way each Resonant unlock permanently changes the way your build plays. You beat a Resonant, the game offers you up to three combat abilities, and you pick one. The others are locked off for the rest of that playthrough. That choice cascades into every encounter from that point on, because the Aberrant's effectiveness at the mid-game wall depends on which branch you committed to and which form pairs with it. I have now done three runs of the launch build with different Resonant picks, and the guide below is the version I wish I had before the first one.

## Resonants Are the Build Pivots

Resonants are former Persons of Power — figures who once held paranatural abilities — corrupted by the force that has warped Manhattan. They are not Hiss or Mold. They are singular antagonists, each tied to their own zone and their own arc. Beating one unlocks up to three new combat abilities, and you pick which one you take. The choice is permanent on a first playthrough. On New Game Plus, you can equip multiple abilities from the same Resonant, which is the main reason NG+ exists as a build playground.

Two Resonants have been publicly named in first-party materials: the Dancer and the Deserter. A third, the Collective, was shown in the gamescom demo in August 2025. Additional Resonants exist; their identities have not been disclosed by Remedy as of launch week.

## The Dancer: First Named Resonant, Best Mobility Build Reward

The Dancer is a masked figure that fights with dual mallets. The arena is wide, the wind-ups are clear, and the tells are short. What makes the Dancer dangerous is the speed: the wide swings close distance faster than most boss attacks, and the spin combinations cover the entire arena in a single animation. If you read the wind-ups and dodge perfectly, the Dancer opens up to heavy Retaliation counters. If you button-mash, the mallets will end you in three hits.

<ProTip title="Phase Shift is the meta pick for solo play">If you are running solo and you have not committed to a specific late-game build, take Phase Shift. The teleport echo lets you skip entire encounter windows in the next act and pairs cleanly with both aggression-focused and defense-focused form loadouts. Counter Tempo and Rallying Beat are stronger in group play or in fights where the boss stays in your face.</ProTip>

The Dancer fight itself wants a Scythe primary to spread Falter across the wide arena. Save the Hammer Combo Ender for the final phase when the Dancer's stagger bar is closest to full. I lost two attempts trying to burst the boss down with Dual Blades; the damage was higher per second but I was trading health constantly and ran out of healing orbs before the second stagger window. The Scythe's wide arcs gave me room to breathe and to chain Perfect Dodges into Retaliation finishers.

## The Deserter: Second Resonant, Best Kiting Build Reward

The Deserter is the slow counterweight to the Dancer. A towering figure barely holding its molten body together, the Deserter turns its factory arena into a hazard zone. Lava pools on the floor, magma spit hits from range, and the red-hot pipes protruding from the body are melee-range damage dealers. You do not stand still. You do not back the boss into a corner. You re-approach from a different angle every fifteen seconds or you die.

Heat Cleave and Lava Lance are stronger damage tools, but they both reward an aggressive playstyle that the Deserter's arena actively punishes.

## The Collective: Third Resonant, The Verticality Test

The Collective is the Resonant that uses the game's verticality most aggressively. It is a heaped mass that forms scowling faces and anchors itself to its arena through tentacles embedded in the surrounding walls. The fight is built around those anchors rather than around the body. If you do not break the anchors, the boss regenerates. If you only attack the body, you lose. The Collective rewards you for thinking with your movement kit, and the Gravity Shift tool is the unlock that makes the fight feel solvable.

My recommendation for a first-playthrough solo build going into the mid-game is Scythe primary, Gauntlets secondary, and Hammer as Combo Ender. The Scythe spreads Falter across every encounter in Act 2 and 3, the Gauntlets keep you mobile when you are running low on healing, and the Hammer's burst lets you execute a Faltered enemy in one hit. If you have unlocked Iron Veil from the Deserter, swap the Gauntlets for Dual Blades — Iron Veil plus the Blade's Power generation is the strongest solo combination I have found.

## New Game Plus: Where the Build Playground Opens Up

The reason New Game Plus exists, beyond the obvious NG+ difficulty bump, is that the Ability-restriction rule relaxes. On your second run, you can equip multiple different Abilities from the same Resonant, which opens synergy combinations that were locked off in the first playthrough. Phase Shift plus Counter Tempo from the Dancer, plus Iron Veil plus Lava Lance from the Deserter, is the most popular combined-Resonant build for NG+. The damage ceiling is higher, the survivability is tighter, and the build rewards precise execution over button-mashing.

The Resonant bosses also get new attack patterns in NG+. The Dancer's spin combos now chain into unblockable finishers; the Deserter's lava pools now persist across phases; the Collective's tentacles now regrow once per phase. The increased pressure rewards defensive Abilities (Iron Veil, Rallying Beat) more than offensive ones, so if you are going into NG+ for the first time, plan to take at least one defensive Ability from your first Resonant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Resonants are in CONTROL Resonant?**
At least three named Resonants are confirmed in the launch build: the Dancer, the Deserter, and the Collective. Additional Resonants exist in the late-game zones and are encountered as story progresses, but Remedy has not published their names or reward structures as of launch week.

**Can I take more than one Ability from the same Resonant?**
No on a first playthrough — you pick one of three and the others are sealed off. Yes on New Game Plus, which is the main reason NG+ exists for buildcraft purposes.

**What is the best Aberrant form for mid-game play?**
Scythe. It has the best AoE Falter spread in the game and pairs with Iron Veil for an unbreakable defensive shell.

**Do I need to play the first Control?**
No. Resonant is designed as both a sequel and an entry point. Playing the 2019 game enriches the lore callbacks but is not required.

**How long is the campaign?**
The main story takes about 30 hours; full completion with side content and Resonant replays runs around 50.

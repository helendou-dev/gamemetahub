Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls launched on August 6, 2026, and after pouring 15+ hours into the full game across the open beta and launch weekend, I have a clear picture of where every character stands. Here's the thing that surprised me most: the roster is more balanced than I expected. There's no day-one Bayonetta situation. But there *are* characters who consistently outperform others, and if you're climbing ranked, you need to know which ones.

This tier list is based on my experience in both casual and ranked matches, cross-referenced with community consensus from the [open beta](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/marvel-tokon-fighting-souls-review) and early competitive sets. I've also factored in team synergy — because in a 4v4 tag fighter, individual strength only tells half the story.

## How This Tier List Works

Marvel Tōkon is a 4v4 tag fighter with a **shared health bar** and progressive character unlocks — you start each match with two active characters and unlock your third and fourth as rounds progress. This means a character's value depends heavily on when they enter the fight and what they bring to team compositions.

I'm ranking characters based on: neutral game strength, damage output, assist quality, ease of execution, and how well they synergize with common team archetypes. Characters who are strong solo but offer poor assists rank lower than characters who elevate the whole team.

## S Tier — The Characters You Ban First

### Star-Lord

Star-Lord is the most complete character in the current meta. His Element Gun switches between fire, ice, and electric modes, giving him answers to nearly every situation. What pushes him into S-tier is his ability to convert small hits into full team extensions through Elemental Grenade setups — you get one clean hit and suddenly your entire team is comboing.

I spent my first five hours trying to main Spider-Man before switching to Star-Lord, and the difference was immediate. Star-Lord's neutral game is forgiving: you have projectiles for zoning, mobility for escapes, and his Quick Skill inputs are genuinely useful rather than training wheels. If you want one character to learn inside and out, pick him.

### Black Panther

Black Panther was the biggest standout of the open beta, and nothing in the full release changed that. His pressure game is suffocating once he gets momentum — fast dashes, cross-up potential, and a Blast Blessing gauge that rewards aggressive play with bonus damage. He doesn't need setup time or specific team comps to function; he walks in, establishes dominance, and forces opponents to react.

The weakness? If you lose neutral against heavy zoning characters like Doctor Doom or Iron Man, Black Panther struggles to close the gap. But in a game where aggression is king, his speed wins more matchups than it loses.

### Blade

Blade's versatility is his defining trait. He seamlessly switches between gun and sword attacks, making him effective at both mid-range and close-range. His transitions feel natural rather than clunky — you can fire a few shots to approach, then commit to a sword combo when you're in range.

What I learned the hard way: Blade's assist is one of the best in the game for extending combos. If you slot him as a secondary character rather than your point, his gun assist creates openings that your main can exploit. I initially tried running him as point and felt underwhelmed; moving him to the second slot transformed my team.

### Magik

Magik is the highest-execution character in S-tier, and that's the only thing keeping her from being universally dominant. Her Soulsword combos deal massive damage, and her portal mechanics allow for creative mix-ups that most players haven't fully explored yet. In the right hands, she deletes health bars.

But — and this is important — Magik requires real lab time. I tried her for two hours and felt like I was playing a different game. Her teleport cancels and portal setups demand precise timing that casual players won't invest. If you're willing to put in the practice, she's S-tier. If you're not, she drops to B. Be honest with yourself about which player you are.

## A Tier — Strong, Reliable, One Weakness Away from S

### Spider-Man

Spider-Man thrives on speed and unpredictability. His Web-Swing resets create constant pressure loops, and Maximum Spider forces stage transitions that directly impact team momentum. In theory, he should be S-tier.

In practice, I found that his damage output doesn't match his speed. You work twice as hard for the same damage that Star-Lord gets from a single combo. He's still excellent — his mobility alone wins matchups — but he's not the carry character that his popularity suggests. If you're picking Spider-Man because he's Spider-Man, that's fine. Just know you're choosing style over efficiency.

### Ghost Rider

Ghost Rider controls space in a way no other character can match. His chains aren't just zoning tools — they actively disrupt assist calls, which is huge in a team-heavy system. If the enemy team relies on assist pressure (and most do), Ghost Rider shuts that down.

His weakness is mobility. Once a rushdown character like Wolverine or Black Panther gets inside his chain range, Ghost Rider has limited options. Pair him with a character who can cover his close-range gap and he's A-tier bordering on S.

### Captain America

Captain America is the most stable, balanced character on the roster. He doesn't excel at any one thing, but he has no real weaknesses either. His shield provides both offense and defense, his combos are straightforward, and he functions in any team slot.

I recommend Cap to every new player, and I'll do it again here: if you're new to fighting games, start with Captain America. He teaches you fundamentals without punishing you for not knowing frame-perfect combos. You can read more about why in our [Marvel Tōkon Beginner's Guide](/games/marvel-tokon/beginners-guide).

### Doctor Doom

Doom's assist game is top-tier. His shield projectile blocks enemy attacks while dealing damage, and his Hidden Missiles and Plasma Beam define neutral control for many team compositions. He's more valuable as a support character than a point character — his assists create the openings that your damage dealers exploit.

The catch: his solo damage is mediocre. If Doom is your last character standing, you're in trouble. Always pair him with at least one character who can close out a match alone.

### Wolverine

Wolverine is a pure rushdown threat. Berserker Rage adds armor to key moves, letting him push through trades and maintain pressure. His healing factor gives him slightly more survivability than other melee characters, which matters in long sets.

His weakness is approach. Without a projectile or strong mobility tool, Wolverine struggles against zoning-heavy teams. He needs an assist that covers his approach — Star-Lord's Elemental Grenade or Captain America's shield throw work well.

### Iron Man

Iron Man is the reliable all-rounder. His Repulsor Blast is a strong ranged attack, his free flight lets him control airspace, and his combo routes are consistent. He's the character you pick when you're not sure what the enemy team is running.

That said, he gets rushed down hard. Once a fast character gets inside his zoning range, Iron Man has limited escape options. He's best in the second slot where he can control neutral without being the first target.

## B Tier — Good, But With Clear Limitations

### Storm

Storm dominates the skies. Her lightning attacks, floating movement, and outstanding aerial control make her frustrating to chase. But her lower durability means mistakes are punished much harder than other characters. One bad read and she loses 30% of the shared health bar.

### Magneto

Magneto's debris-based attacks are effective and satisfying, but his overall damage output doesn't match the top tiers. He's viable in the right team comp, but I wouldn't recommend him as a primary character.

### Hulk

Nobody survives longer than Hulk. His health pool is enormous and his hyper armor lets him ignore many attacks. But his huge hurtbox makes extended combos against him almost effortless for skilled opponents. He's a beginner-friendly pick who falls off at higher ranks.

### Green Goblin

The Goblin Glider creates unique aerial movement, and pumpkin bombs constantly interrupt approaches. But corner pressure exposes his biggest weakness — escaping is nearly impossible once opponents close the distance. I tried maining Green Goblin for a full day and gave up; his win condition is too binary.

### Ms. Marvel

Ms. Marvel has fantastic reach and is beginner-friendly. Unfortunately, experienced players punish her lengthy recovery animations consistently. She's a great learning tool but not a long-term main for competitive play.

## C Tier — Niche Specialists

### Deadpool

Deadpool is fun. His fourth-wall-breaking moveset is entertaining and his combos have style. But "fun" doesn't win ranked matches. His damage output is inconsistent and his tools are outclassed by other characters who do similar things better. If you're playing casually, go wild. If you're climbing ranked, pick someone else.

### Loki

Loki rewards patience. Clone traps, counter mechanics, and mind games make him a nightmare for impatient opponents. But rushdown specialists can overwhelm him before his setup begins. In a meta that favors aggression, Loki is fighting uphill.

### Carnage

Carnage has the highest risk-reward ratio in the game. His offense explodes once it connects. Actually getting that first clean hit is another story — without defensive armor or consistently safe approaches, experienced opponents punish his commitment extremely hard.

### Danger

Danger's adaptive mechanics and laser control are interesting in theory. In practice, her movement is noticeably slower than most of the roster, which becomes painfully obvious against elite rushdown characters.

## D Tier — Situational at Best

### Peni Parker

Peni Parker relies on summoning abilities that deal relatively low damage and make her feel slow during fights. Opponents can easily block or destroy her summons and punish her with quick attacks. In a fast-paced 4v4 environment, she rarely gets enough breathing room to establish her game plan.

I wanted Peni to work because the SP//dr mech concept is cool. But after ten matches, I had to accept that she's the weakest character in the launch roster. Maybe a future patch will help. Until then, only pick her if you're significantly better than your opponents.

## Team Composition Matters More Than Tiers

Here's the most important thing I learned: **individual tier placement matters less than team synergy in Marvel Tōkon.** I've watched skilled Magneto, Blade, and even Hulk players dismantle supposedly "better" teams because they understood spacing, timing, and assists on a deeper level.

## The PC Performance Elephant in the Room

I need to address this because it affects character evaluations: the PC port has serious performance issues. Players with high-end GPUs are reporting frame drops and slowdown, primarily caused by Sony's PSSR software. The PS5 version runs at a stable 60fps with no issues, but if you're on PC, your experience may vary. This doesn't change tier placements, but it does mean some characters with tight frame links (Magik, Blade) are harder to play on a stuttering PC.

## FAQ

### Who is the best character in Marvel Tōkon for beginners?

Captain America. He has straightforward combos, strong defensive tools, and no execution barriers. Ms. Marvel and Wolverine are also solid beginner picks. Check our [beginner's guide](/games/marvel-tokon/beginners-guide) for a full breakdown.

### Is the tier list final?

No. The game launched two days ago. Tier lists will shift dramatically as players discover new tech, team compositions, and optimal routes. Treat this as a launch snapshot, not gospel.

### How many characters are in Marvel Tōkon?

20 at launch, with 4 more coming through the Year 1 Character and Stage Pass. The first DLC character is Phoenix Cyclops.

### Does Marvel Tōkon have cross-play?

Yes. PS5 and PC players share the same matchmaking pool in both casual and ranked modes.

### Is Marvel Tōkon on Xbox or Switch?

No. It's a PS5 console exclusive with a simultaneous PC release. No other platforms have been announced.

### Should I pick based on tier list or personal preference?

Both. If you love a character, you'll put in the lab time to make them work. But if you're purely optimizing for wins, the S-tier characters have clear advantages in the current meta. My advice: pick one S-tier character to learn fundamentals, then branch out to whoever you enjoy most.

## Final Thoughts

Marvel Tōkon's launch roster is more balanced than I expected, but the gaps are real. Star-Lord, Black Panther, Blade, and Magik consistently outperform the rest of the cast — not because they're broken, but because their tools are more versatile and their execution requirements are more rewarding.

The good news? Arc System Works has a long history of meaningful balance updates. The tier list you see today will look different in three months. So pick a character you genuinely enjoy playing, learn their matchups, and don't stress too much about the letter next to their name. Skill beats tier placement in this game more than most fighters — and that's a good thing.

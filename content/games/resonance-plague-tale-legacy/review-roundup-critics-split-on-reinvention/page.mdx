**Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy** launches today, August 27, on PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S — day one on Game Pass — and the review embargo has lifted on the most divisive entry Asobo Studio has ever shipped. The prequel currently sits at **79 on Metacritic** (85 on OpenCritic), behind both Requiem (82) and Innocence (81), and the spread of scores tells the real story: this is a game reviewers either love as a bold action-adventure pivot or bounce off as a shallow, misery-drenched departure from the series' stealth roots.

Here's where every major outlet landed, and what the split actually means for your first session.

## The Fans: A Genuine Reinvention

The strongest reviews argue the switch from rats-and-stealth to treasure-hunting combat was overdue. Game Informer's Kyle Hilliard gave it **8.5/10**, praising protagonist Sophia — "strong, smart, and like many of the pirates in her community, I questioned why she wasn't already in charge" — and calling the performances excellent in both dramatic and quiet moments. [Metro awarded **8/10**](https://metro.co.uk/2026/08/26/resonance-a-plague-tale-legacy-escaping-rat-race-29469400/) and reported a flawless technical run: no crashes, no glitches across a 17-hour playthrough, with combat that "gets more interesting with the addition of special moves and more enemies." TheXboxHub went even higher at **4.5/5**, calling it "a spellbinding adventure that successfully mixes A Plague Tale and its universe with Greek mythology," despite combat that "does outstay its welcome a little."

## The Skeptics: Shallow Combat, Excessive Grimness

The other side is sharp. IGN's Travis Northup gave **6/10**, arguing the shift from stealth to open combat "isn't the most seamless transition — not exactly bad, but consistently shallow and sometimes sloppy," with enemies offering just "two or three different attacks to learn." Eurogamer's Chris Schilling landed at **3/5** with different complaints: the Tomb Raider-style exploration worked, but the storytelling "ladles on the trauma in great heaping portions," a "full-on descent into abject misery." Polygon's Willa Rowe was similarly middling — "deeply boring" action paired with world design and performances strong enough to recommend it as an Uncharted-style adventure, just not a Plague Tale one.

## What the Split Means for You

The consensus reading: if you're coming for more of Amicia and Hugo's slow-burn stealth, this isn't it — expect sword combat, light-beam puzzles, and a Minoan Minotaur retelling instead. If you're happy with a polished action-adventure in the Tomb Raider mold with a superb voice cast (Anna Demetriou's Sophia is singled out everywhere), you'll likely land with the 8s.

Game Pass is the deciding factor for most people: at zero additional cost, this is an easy weekend pick-up regardless of the score wars. On the other hand, if you're paying the $49.99 PC / £49.99 console price, the IGN and Eurogamer takes carry more weight — this is a game whose value hinges on whether the combat clicks with you. ## What to Watch

The interesting number to track is OpenCritic's 85 vs Metacritic's 79 — as harsher reviews get ingested, the gap will narrow. For now, Game Pass subscribers get the cheapest possible answer to whether Asobo's reinvention works, and everyone else gets a genuinely interesting debate about whether a franchise's identity should be allowed to change this much.

Mistfall Hunter launched on July 29, 2026, and within 24 hours it hit 100,000+ players and shot to #1 on Steam's top sellers list, dethroning both Palworld and Halo: Campaign Evolved. Developed by Bellring Games and published by Skystone Games, this dark fantasy PvPvE extraction ARPG blends Soulslike combat with the tension of games like Hunt: Showdown and Dark and Darker.

But one question dominates the community discussion: **can you actually play this solo?**

The short answer is yes — but with significant restrictions that every new player needs to understand before buying.

## Can You Play Mistfall Hunter Solo?

Yes, but "solo" doesn't mean what you might think. Solo mode in Mistfall Hunter is **not** a private single-player campaign. When you enter an expedition alone, you still drop into an online PvPvE map populated by AI enemies *and* other players. There are no AI companions filling your party slots, no option to turn off PvP, and no offline mode.

This is the single biggest source of negative reviews on Steam right now. Many players bought Mistfall Hunter expecting a Soulslike action RPG and discovered an extraction game where death means losing everything you carried in.

Hollowgrove is where you'll spend your early hours. It's the tutorial map and the primary solo destination. Brandergard, the second map, is designed for three-player squads and doesn't have permanent solo matchmaking. A solo variant of Brandergard has been discussed for a future update, but it's not available at launch.

If your goal is to experience the full game solo, you'll hit a wall once you exhaust Hollowgrove's content.

## Best Classes for Solo Play

Not every class works alone. Here's how the six Gyldhunters stack up for solo extraction:

**Mercenary** — The undisputed best solo starter. Sword and shield give you blocks, parries, and crowd control. High health pool means you survive mistakes. Hammer stance adds AoE for mob clearing. If you're new to extraction games, pick this first.

**Sorcerer** — Highest damage output in the game. Elemental spells (fire, ice, thunder) let you delete enemies before they reach you. The catch: you're a glass cannon. One boss combo can end your run. Maintain distance, use teleport to escape, and you'll extract consistently.

**Blackarrow** — Excellent for cautious solo players. Bow range lets you pick fights and apply bleed/poison from safety. Shadow Step provides brief stealth for repositioning. Weakness: if a melee class gets in your face, you're done. Requires precise aim.

**Withered Knight** — Viable but risky solo. The Withered debuff mechanic is powerful, and the grappling hook adds mobility. High-risk, high-reward playstyle that punishes mistakes hard. Better for experienced players.

**Shadowstrix** — S-tier in PvP, but rough solo in PvE. Stealth attacks are devastating against other players, but the class struggles against boss mobs and groups. Low health means one bad engagement ends your run. Not recommended for solo beginners.

**Seer** — Avoid for solo. This is a support healer designed for coordinated trios. Without teammates to heal and protect, your damage output is too low to handle PvE threats efficiently. Only pick this if you have a dedicated group.

## Solo Extraction Tips

1. **Extract early, extract often.** The #1 cause of lost runs is greed. Once your inventory has value, leave. One more chest is not worth one more ambush.

2. **Stick to map edges.** The center of Hollowgrove attracts squads and bosses. Loot around the perimeter where traffic is lower, then spiral inward as you learn the map.

3. **Lock doors behind you.** It buys seconds against pursuing players — and in extraction games, seconds are the difference between banking your loot and losing it all.

4. **Upgrade the Goddess Statue first.** It boosts your Springhorn healing. At camp, your upgrade priority should be: Goddess Statue → Warehouse → Tavern → Shop.

5. **Send Scavenger Squad before logging off.** Passive material income means you progress even when you're not playing. Don't skip this.

## Should You Start Solo or Squad?

Start solo if you've played extraction games before and enjoy stealth-based, high-tension gameplay where every decision matters. You'll learn maps faster without squad chaos, and the tension of surviving alone is genuinely thrilling.

Start with a squad if you're completely new to extraction games, want faster progression, or prefer teamwork over solo tension. The game is clearly designed around trio play — boss fights, map variety, and class synergy all point to squads being the intended experience.

The good news? You can switch freely. Queue solo one run, squad up the next. Your inventory and progression carry across both modes. Many experienced players recommend learning maps solo first, then transitioning to squads for farming once you're comfortable with extraction timing.

Whatever you choose, remember the core rule of Mistfall Hunter: **greed is what kills you**. Extract with less, survive more often, and the Gyldenblood will stack up faster than you think.

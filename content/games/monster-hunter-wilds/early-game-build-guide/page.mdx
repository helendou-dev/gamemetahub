I have 80 hours in Monster Hunter Wilds, and about 20 of those were wasted on bad decisions in the first 10. I crafted weapons I never used. I built armor that became obsolete after two hunts. I spent Zenny on items I could have gathered for free. If I could go back and tell my hour-1 self what to do differently, this article is exactly what I'd say.

With the [recent 1.042 patch](/games/monster-hunter-wilds/patch-1-042-notes) smoothing out quality-of-life issues and making event quests playable offline, there has never been a better time to jump into Wilds. But the gear system is deep, and the wrong early choices can set you back hours of grinding. Here is exactly what to build, what to skip, and what your loadout should look like after your first 10 hours.

## Pick Your Weapon: 4 Beginner Picks, Skip the Trap Weapons

Monster Hunter Wilds has 14 weapon types. Four of them are genuinely good for beginners. The other ten range from "learn these later" to "you will hate this game if you start here."

### Sword and Shield — The Undisputed Best Starter Weapon

I dismissed SnS at first because it looked boring. I was wrong. This weapon lets you use items without sheathing, which means you can heal mid-combo while every other weapon user is frantically running away. The shield blocks most early monster attacks with zero timing required, and the Perfect Rush combo does surprising damage once you learn the timing.

If you have never played a Monster Hunter game before, **start with Sword and Shield**. You will cart less, learn monster patterns faster, and actually enjoy your first 10 hours instead of fighting the controls.

This is also the weapon that taught me that defense is damage — every second you spend healing is a second you are not attacking. SnS eliminates that downtime almost entirely.

### Long Sword — The Timing-Based Alternative

Long Sword is the most popular weapon in the series for a reason. The basic combo is easy to execute, the Spirit Gauge damage ramp feels rewarding, and the Foresight Slash counter is one of the most satisfying moves in any action game. I landed my first Foresight Slash against a Chatacabra and immediately understood why people main this weapon for thousands of hours.

The catch: Long Sword demands you learn monster attack patterns. If you just mash buttons, you will never reach red Spirit Gauge, and your damage will be terrible. If you are willing to spend 30 minutes in the Training Area practicing counters before your first real hunt, Long Sword rewards that investment more than any other beginner weapon.

### Dual Blades — Pure Aggression, High Risk

Dual Blades are the fastest weapon in the game. Demon Mode turns you into a blender, and the mobility lets you stick to a monster's legs like glue. I love these for fast monsters like Balahara, where slower weapons feel like you are fighting the camera as much as the monster.

The downside: Dual Blades burn through sharpness faster than any other weapon, and you have zero defensive options beyond dodging. You will spend a lot of time sharpening mid-hunt, which feels awful when you are trying to maintain Demon Mode uptime. Pick these if you come from fast action games like Nioh or Devil May Cry.

### Hammer — The Simple KO Machine

Hammer has one job: hit the monster's head until it falls over. The charged attacks are satisfying, the sliding aerial spin is genuinely fun, and a stunned monster is free damage for everyone. I bring Hammer to every multiplayer hunt because one well-timed KO can turn a 20-minute fight into a 12-minute stomp.

The downside: Hammer's range is short, and you cannot cut tails. If you need tail materials for crafting, you will need a second weapon. But for learning monster openings and contributing to group hunts, Hammer is the most immediately useful blunt weapon.

### Skip These as Your First Weapon

Charge Blade, Insect Glaive, Gunlance, and Hunting Horn all have additional resource management systems layered on top of the base combat. Charge Blade requires you to manage phial charges and Guard Points. Insect Glaive demands Kinsect management. Gunlance adds shelling types and heat management. Hunting Horn requires you to memorize song sequences while fighting.

These are not bad weapons. They are bad **first** weapons. Learn the game with one of the four picks above, then experiment with the complex weapons once you understand monster behavior and item management fundamentals.

## Early Armor That Actually Matters

Most early game armor sets in Monster Hunter Wilds are trap purchases. They cost materials you need for weapon upgrades, and they get replaced after one or two story quests. Here are the only two sets worth crafting before you reach the mid-game.

### Chatacabra Set — Your First Real Armor

I crafted the Chatacabra set after my third hunt and wore it for the next 15 hours. The Speed Eating skill is what makes this set valuable — it cuts your potion drinking animation by roughly 40%, which means you spend less time vulnerable and more time attacking. For a new player who is going to take hits and need to heal often, Speed Eating is the single best survival skill in the early game.

The full set requires 4 Chatacabra hunts on average. Do the optional quest version twice, break the head and forelegs each time, and you will have enough materials. Skip the waist piece if you want to save time — the 4-piece bonus is not essential, and 3 pieces already give you Speed Eating Level 2.

### Balahara Chest + Arms — The Cheap Damage Spike

Balahara armor is ugly — it looks like you wrapped yourself in a dead eel — but the chest piece grants Attack Boost Level 1, and the arms give Evade Extender Level 1. Combined with the Chatacabra head and legs, this mixed set gives you Speed Eating, Attack Boost, and Evade Extender by hour 5. That is more skill synergy than most players have at hour 20.

I skipped the Balahara waist and legs entirely. The waist skill (Water Resistance) is useless against most early monsters, and the legs require a Balahara Plate, which has a 3% drop rate. Do not farm for plates in the early game. It is the single biggest time-waster I see new players fall into.

According to the <a href="https://monsterhunterwilds.wiki.fextralife.com/Armor" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">community armor database on Fextralife</a>, early game mixed sets consistently outperform full monster sets until you reach the Guardian-tier monsters at roughly story rank 5. The Chatacabra-Balahara mix I am recommending matches what speedrunners used during the first week of launch.

## First 10 Hours Progression Path

Here is exactly what I would do if I started a fresh save tomorrow:

**Hours 0-2: Weapon Trials and Chatacabra Farming**
Pick one weapon from the four above. Spend 30 minutes in the Training Area — not 5 minutes, actually 30. Learn every combo listed in the Play Guide. Then hunt Chatacabra 3-4 times. By the end of hour 2, you should have your weapon's basic moveset memorized and a 3-piece Chatacabra set equipped.

**Hours 2-5: Story Progression to Balahara**
Push the main story until you unlock the Balahara hunt. Do not get distracted by optional quests yet — the story gates your camp unlocks, and camps are fast travel points that save you minutes of travel time per hunt. Hunt Balahara 2-3 times for the chest and arms. You now have a functional mixed set by hour 5.

**Hours 5-8: Weapon Upgrade + Item Economy**
By now you should have enough materials for your first weapon upgrade. Prioritize raw damage over elemental damage — early monsters have low elemental hitzones, and raw damage is universally effective. While hunting, gather every Herb, Honey, and Blue Mushroom you see. Your Seikret can auto-gather these while you ride.

Start crafting Mega Potions (Potion + Honey) and keep 10 in your inventory at all times. This seems obvious, but I spent my first 10 hours chugging regular Potions like a fool because I did not want to "waste" Honey. Honey is everywhere. Use it.

**Hours 8-10: First Real Challenge**
By hour 8, the story will throw your first difficulty spike at you — usually a multiple-monster quest or your first Guardian-tier fight. This is where your gear choices get tested. If you have the Chatacabra-Balahara mixed set and a once-upgraded weapon, you will clear it. If you skipped armor crafting and are still wearing the starter gear with an unupgraded weapon, you will hit a wall.

I hit this wall on my first playthrough and spent 3 hours grinding materials I should have gathered naturally. On my second save, I breezed through the same fight in 18 minutes. The difference was entirely in preparation.

## The Seikret Is Your Mobile Base — Stop Ignoring It

The Seikret is new to Wilds, and I ignored it for my first 5 hours because I thought it was just a horse. Much like how picking the wrong [Elden Ring build](/games/elden-ring/best-builds-tier-list-2025) can make your first 20 hours miserable, ignoring the Seikret's utility makes every hunt harder than it needs to be. It is not. The Seikret is a mobile item box, a weapon-switching station, and an escape tool all in one.

**Sharpen on the move.** While riding the Seikret, you can use your Whetstone without stopping. This alone cuts 3-5 seconds of vulnerability from every sharpening cycle, which adds up to minutes of saved time per hunt.

**Restock mid-hunt.** If you run out of Mega Potions, call your Seikret and access your item box from the saddle. This is especially valuable on longer hunts where the monster changes zones multiple times and you burn through healing items.

**Switch weapons on the fly.** The Seikret carries a secondary weapon. I keep a Hammer on my Seikret when I am running Long Sword, so I can swap to blunt damage whenever the monster's head is exposed. This flexibility is unique to Wilds and I did not use it until hour 30, which was a mistake.

**Auto-pursue.** Set the Seikret to auto-follow the monster while you craft items, check the map, or just take a breath. It sounds minor, but in practice it means you arrive at the next fight zone with full stamina, sharpened weapon, and restocked items — every single time.

## Focus Mode: When to Use It (and When Not To)

Focus Mode highlights weak points on the monster and lets you aim your attacks precisely. It is powerful, but it has a resource gauge, and wasting Focus Strikes on non-weak points is a common beginner mistake.

**Use Focus Mode when:** the monster is staggered, knocked down, or trapped. This is when you can land Focus Strikes on exposed weak points without the monster moving. A Focus Strike on a wounded head part deals roughly double the damage of a normal combo finisher.

**Do not use Focus Mode when:** the monster is mobile and aggressive. Activating Focus Mode slows your character slightly, and against fast monsters like Balahara or the Guardian variants, that half-second of slowdown will get you hit. Focus Mode is an offensive tool for damage windows, not a defensive crutch.

I learned this the hard way against Guardian Rathalos. I kept trying to land Focus Strikes mid-fight, got fireballed three times in a row, and carted in under 4 minutes. On the rematch, I saved Focus Mode for the knockdowns and cleared the fight with zero carts.

## Common Mistakes I Made (So You Do Not Have To)

### 1. Farming for Rare Materials Too Early

Monster Hunter veteran players know that Plates and Gems have abysmal drop rates — typically 2-5% from carves. I spent 2 hours farming Balahara for a Plate in my first playthrough, only to learn that the Plate is used for a single armor piece I did not even need. Do not farm for rare materials until you reach High Rank. Low Rank gear gets replaced too quickly for the time investment to be worth it.

### 2. Ignoring Meal Buffs

Meals in Wilds are not optional flavor — they grant 30-minute buffs to max health, stamina, attack, and defense. I skipped meals for my first 8 hours because I did not understand the BBQ grill system. I was fighting monsters with 100 HP that hit for 40 damage per attack. With a meal buff, that same hunter has 150 HP, and suddenly you survive three hits instead of two. Cook before every hunt. Set the BBQ grill to your radial menu so you never forget.

### 3. Upgrading the Wrong Weapon Path

Every weapon has branching upgrade trees, and some paths lead to dead ends with terrible sharpness or useless elemental damage. The Bone tree is consistently the best early game path for raw damage weapons — it upgrades faster, requires common materials, and has better sharpness than the Ore alternatives for most weapon types. Check the upgrade tree preview before committing materials. A single wrong upgrade can waste 3-4 hunts worth of materials.

The <a href="https://www.capcom-games.com/mhwilds/en-us/weapon/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official Capcom weapon page</a> lists the base stats for every weapon type, which is useful for comparing upgrade paths before you craft.

## Verdict: Your Loadout at Hour 10

If you followed this guide, here is what your hunter should look like after 10 hours:

- **Weapon**: Sword and Shield, Long Sword, Dual Blades, or Hammer — once upgraded on the Bone path
- **Armor**: Chatacabra Head + Chest + Legs, Balahara Arms + Chest (mixed set)
- **Skills active**: Speed Eating Lv2, Attack Boost Lv1, Evade Extender Lv1
- **Secondary weapon** on Seikret: Hammer (if using a blade weapon) or Long Sword (if using Hammer)
- **Item loadout**: 10 Mega Potions, 2 Max Potions, Antidotes, Well-Done Steaks, Whetstones
- **Meal buff**: Active before every hunt
- **Camps**: All available story camps unlocked

This loadout is not optimal for speedrunning. It is not the meta DPS setup. But it will get you through the first 10 hours without hitting a gear wall, and every material you spent crafting it is easily replaceable once you reach the mid-game.

The real test is whether you enjoy the hunts. I failed my first Guardian Rathalos because I skipped armor upgrades. I succeeded on my second save because I built the Chatacabra-Balahara set at hour 4. The difference was not skill — it was preparation. And if there is one thing Monster Hunter teaches you, it is that preparation beats reflexes every time.

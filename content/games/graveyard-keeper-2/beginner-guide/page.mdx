Graveyard Keeper 2 is out. Eight years after Lazy Bear Games turned running a medieval cemetery into the most unlikely management-game hit of 2018, the sequel lands on every platform at once today — PC (Steam, Epic, GOG), PlayStation 5 and PS4, Xbox Series X|S and Xbox One, plus Nintendo Switch and Switch 2 — all for $24.99 standard or $19.99 on the still-active 20% pre-order discount.

If you are coming from the original Graveyard Keeper — or if you only just downloaded the [free Steam demo](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4358690/Graveyard_Keeper_2/) that [TinyBuild Connect 2026 dropped on September 14](http://gamers6.com/indie/graveyard-keeper-2s-free-demo-lands-with-a-save-carry-into-the-september-22-launch) — the headline change is structural. You are still a graveyard keeper, but now you are also the Grand Inquisitor, the town is broken, and the world outside your fence is overrun with zombies. The opening hours still feel like a graveyard sim. The mid-game starts to feel like an undead logistics company. The late game is closer to a tower-defense commander running a courier in production. This guide covers the first 14 days on the launch build, which is the window where your save is most likely to be quietly ruined by a bad early unlock or a corpse that should have been reanimated.

Your demo save transfers into the full game. That is genuinely important: Lazy Bear confirmed save carryover at launch, so any decisions in the three to four-hour demo carry through. Use the first week deliberately.

## What Has Changed from the Original Graveyard Keeper

The core loop survives: bury the dead, harvest bodies, grow crops, run an economy, automate what you can, ignore the moral implications. The big new systems are [Graveyard Keeper 2's stack so you can verify exact details](https://www.xmodhub.com/info/guides/graveyard-keeper-2-walkthrough) against the launch build:

- **Town restoration.** A whole second map district you unlock once your cemetery is humming. You help residents, rebuild houses, and — because this is still Graveyard Keeper — turn every favour into a margin.
- **Zombie workforce.** Reanimated corpses that automate production. The original teased this; the sequel ships it.
- **Combat.** You build fortifications and fight. The opening hours stay civilian, but the second map is a war zone.
- **Anatomy-first corpse handling.** The original opened with full dissection; the sequel [opens with cleaner corpse examination](https://yomimono.id/graveyard-keeper-2-demo-shows-zombie-army-battles-and-town-restoration) and works up to the gruesome bits.

The economy underneath still runs on time, energy, and the question "do I bury this or do I work it?" Both answers are valid. The trick is knowing which one.

## The Tech Point System: Red, Green, Blue

Almost every action generates some kind of Tech Point — you cannot grind one colour in isolation. Some blueprints are also gated by story quests and NPC relationships, not just point totals. Plan for that.

<ProTip title="Wood Processing First, Always">

If you only do one thing on day one, unlock wood processing in the Tech Tree. Wood and planks are required by almost every early recipe and building — fences, the carpentry bench, the church study table, the crematorium, the first zombie-reanimation kit. Running short on wood blocks your progress in every other direction.

</ProTip>

The other day-one unlock to chase is the **pottery quest**, because completing it unlocks fishing. Fishing is the single biggest early-game jump in food quality per unit of effort, and food quality is what gates every other activity.

<InfoBox title="Blue Tech Points on the Launch Build">

For the launch build, the [Pro Game Guides walkthrough](https://progameguides.com/graveyard-keeper-2/complete-graveyard-keeper-2-beginners-guide) confirms: the church study table is your primary blue-tech engine. Build the desk, study anatomical parts (a fresh heart or intestine is the highest-yield research material — roughly 50 blue points per item), and skip studying low-value objects like basic wooden markers. Polished stone crosses are worth about 30 blue points each, which makes them a useful mid-game side bet when you need to research something to upgrade your cemetery rating.

</InfoBox>

## Energy and Insanity: The Two Meters That Actually Matter

Two meters quietly gate your entire economy. Most beginners lose hours to them without noticing why.

**Energy** is your action budget. You restore it by eating or sleeping.

- Mushrooms and berries restore very little per unit. Cooked food like bread or fish restores a lot more. Unlocking one good cooking recipe and the fishing rod on day one or two is the single biggest productivity unlock in the early game.
- Sleep works, but it costs daytime cycles. Use sleep as a fallback, not a plan.

**Insanity** is the purple brain icon in the top-left HUD, next to Energy. It climbs mainly from handling corpses, even from routine crafting. The moment it hits a threshold, your maximum Energy drops. Recovery becomes harder. You spiral.

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Meter</th><th>Climbs From</th><th>Effect</th><th>How to Lower It</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Energy</td><td>Any action</td><td>Out of actions</td><td>Food (best), Sleep (slow)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Insanity</td><td>Corpse handling, sustained crafting</td><td>Reduces max Energy, makes recovery harder</td><td>Beer from the Tavern (best), Sleep (only when tired)</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<CommonMistake title="Wait Until You Are Hungry to Eat">

The most common mistake is treating food reactively — eating only when the Energy bar is empty. By then, Insanity is climbing, your maximum Energy has already dropped, and you have to sleep for two days to recover. Eat proactively. Keep food in your inventory. Snack before energy dips, not after.

</CommonMistake>

A working day-one routine: gather 20 berries and harvest 3 bee hives early, cook what you can, and keep the honey for the longer expeditions. Honey restores a large slice of Energy, which means you can spend the rest of the day chopping, mining, or doing autopsies without sprinting back to bed every hour.

## Corpse Quality: White Skulls, Red Skulls, and When Not to Bury

The original's corpse rating has been reworked into a two-axis system: **White Skulls (purity)** and **Red Skulls (corruption)**. They cancel each other out for a net burial score — but they matter separately for two very different decisions.

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Body Trait</th><th>What It Means</th><th>What You Should Do</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>High White Skull net</td><td>Clean, well-prepared body</td><td>Bury it. Boosts your cemetery rating, opens late-game dialogue.</td></tr>
<tr><td>High Red Skull count</td><td>Corrupted body, fetid, dangerous</td><td>Do not bury it. Reanimate it as a zombie worker.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Balanced low net</td><td>Middling body</td><td>Burn it at the crematorium for faith or skip.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

This is the central decision the sequel asks you to make every time a body arrives: **bury, burn, or reanimate?**

<ProTip title="Strip the Blood and Fat First">

Every autopsy should start with the same two moves: remove the blood, then remove the fat. This almost always produces two extra White Skulls per body — it is the single highest-leverage action in the autopsy minigame, and the one most beginners skip because it looks like a waste of an early step.

</ProTip>

The launch build rule is simple: **do not bury a high-Red-Skull corpse in a grave when it could instead become a stronger zombie worker.** The cemetery rating wants pure bodies; the production loop wants corrupted ones. Once you realise those two pools exist, the whole economy reorganises around it.

## The Zombie Workforce: When It Unlocks and What It Does

Zombie automation is the sequel's headline feature. In the launch build it unlocks partway through the demo-to-full-game progression via the reanimation branch of the Tech Tree.

Once you can reanimate:

- Bodies with high Red Skull counts become your best workers. A heavily corrupted corpse is more obedient and tougher to kill on the job — exactly what you want for a long shift in the quarry or the wood yard.
- Assign zombie workers to stations the same way you assign living NPCs: quarries, farms, workshops, transport routes. They never sleep, never eat, and do not need beer to keep Insanity in check.
- The undead do not pay you, do not leave for greener pastures, and (once you have built the production chain) do not need hand-holding.

The catch is that setting up a reanimation station and the support chain to feed it costs wood, stone, and several Tech Tree nodes that you would otherwise have spent on farming or buildings. It is not a day-one investment. It is a day-five-to-day-ten investment, depending on how much body flow you have generated by that point.

<InfoBox title="How to Know You Are Ready">

If you are generating corpses faster than you can bury them without dropping cemetery rating, you are ready for the reanimation tech. If you can still comfortably bury every body and your cemetery rating is climbing, hold off on reanimation until the town loop opens up.

</InfoBox>

## Town Restoration: The Second Half of the Loop

Town restoration is unlocked after the early-game milestone that ends the demo, and it is the structural change that makes Graveyard Keeper 2 feel bigger than its predecessor. You rebuild houses, help residents, and — because this is still Graveyard Keeper — turn every favour into a margin.

A few confirmed facts about how the loop fits with the cemetery side:

- The town unlocks new customers for your cemetery products and new reasons to ship bodies.
- Upgrading town markers (replacing broken wooden markers with polished stone crosses) directly raises your cemetery rating. The two economies are not separate. They are one big system.
- Helping residents is not charity. Every town quest is also a transaction. The 2018 original was already cynical; the sequel is more so.

## Seven Beginner Mistakes That Cost You Hours

These are the recurring ways I have seen new saves collapse on the launch build. The Pro Game Guides and XMODhub walkthroughs both flag versions of each.

1. **Skipping the honey and berry loop.** Energy starvation forces you to sleep half the day. The fix is 20 berries and 3 hives a day, every day, from day one.
2. **Letting Insanity climb past the warning line.** One bad autopsy day and your max Energy is permanently lower for a full in-game week. Keep beer stocked.
3. **Burying a high-Red-Skull body.** That was your strongest possible zombie worker. Bury the clean one, reanimate the corrupted one.
4. **Forgetting to strip blood and fat first in autopsies.** Two free White Skulls, every time. The single highest-leverage autopsy move.
5. **Pushing the northern map without a stone cutter.** The launch build drains stamina off-route until you have the right tool. Turn back.
6. **Studying wooden markers for blue points.** A heart or intestine is roughly 50 points; a basic wooden marker is near zero. Reserve the church study table for high-yield items.
7. **Treating Inspiration like a side goal.** Cutting trees, gathering mushrooms, and crafting all fill the Inspiration bar. When it converts into a star point, you spend it on perks. The perks feed your Mastery, which makes every action faster. It is not a separate goal; it is a passive accelerator.

## Is Graveyard Keeper 2 Multiplayer?

No. Graveyard Keeper 2 is single-player only. The store page and the [Pro Game Guides FAQ](https://progameguides.com/graveyard-keeper-2/complete-graveyard-keeper-2-beginners-guide) both confirm there is no co-op or online multiplayer. Plan your session length accordingly — this is a 50+ hour single-player save.

## The Bottom Line

Graveyard Keeper 2 keeps the slow, slightly grotesque management loop that made the original a sleeper hit, and bolts onto it a structural second half — the Town — that gives the late game somewhere to go. The first 14 days are the part of the game where you will lose the most time to mistakes: forget the food loop, let Insanity climb, bury the wrong corpses, or push the map before you have the right tools. None of those mistakes are permanent, but each one quietly costs you three or four in-game days.

Get the food loop running on day one. Unlock Wood Processing. Strip the blood and fat on every autopsy. Buy a beer for the post-autopsy. Route corrupted bodies to the zombie workforce, clean bodies to the cemetery. By the time the Town unlocks, your cemetery economy should already be self-sustaining, and the second map will feel like a reward instead of a chore.

## How the Game Actually Works

Every mission follows the same loop: you place turrets on a map, orcs flood toward your base along the shortest path, and your turrets do the shooting. Kills earn crystals. Crystals buy upgrades on a tech tree. Then you run the level again — stronger.

**Losing is farming.** You keep every crystal from failed runs. The devs openly balanced the game around replaying levels a few times before beating them, so don't feel bad about dying to 3.2 six times in a row. That's the design.

The full release ships **12 maps** numbered in a world.stage format (1.1, 2.1, 3.2, 6.1, etc.), with roughly **4 to 6 hours** for a full clear. Scale ramps fast: map one throws about 400 orcs at you, map two hits 2,000, and later maps flood past 100,000.

**The flamethrower is your backbone.** It burns every orc in its sweep arc for damage over time, making it absurdly efficient wherever enemies bunch up. Community testing settled on building entire walls of flamethrowers at the biggest chokepoint on late maps.

**Machine guns are exit insurance.** They fire more accurately the farther out you aim them — the devs designed them for base defense, not forward chokepoints. Place several with overlapping fields of fire right at your exit so the orcs that survive your kill zone can't slip through.

**Cannons pair with flamethrowers.** Cannon shots penetrate through multiple orcs, softening the mass while fire finishes the work. Don't rely on them alone near the exit — they fire too slow for precise final picks.

## Turret Placement: Sweep Arcs Are Everything

The build phase shows each turret's sweep arc as a white cone. **Aim the arc, not the turret.**

Two controls most new players miss:
- **Drag a placed turret** to re-aim its sweep arc without rebuilding
- **Right-click** to delete a turret and get a partial crystal refund

For mortars specifically: the big white circle is the **targeting zone**, not the blast radius. Accuracy drops the farther the mortar sits from that zone. Closer placement = tighter shelling. When upgrading mortars, the community consensus is to focus on radius and explosion damage first.

## Upgrade Order: What to Buy First

The tech tree is massive but the early priority is straightforward:

**Phase 1 — Unlock turret types first.** New towers and extra copies of them beat stat upgrades in the early game. Variety wins over raw numbers. Get flamethrower → machine gun → cannon before buying any damage upgrades.

**Phase 2 — Flamethrower damage.** Once you have one of each core turret, push flamethrower damage and burn duration. This scales your entire kill zone.

**Phase 3 — Laser unlock.** The level 3.2 difficulty wall gets demolished by lasers. If you're stuck grinding around that point, redirect all crystals toward the laser unlock before pushing further stat upgrades.

**Phase 4 — Active abilities.** Airstrike, orbital laser, and nuke are panic buttons. Unlock them but don't rely on them — they have cooldowns and your turrets should be doing the heavy lifting.

## Beating the Difficulty Walls

**Level 3.2 is the first real wall.** Community consensus: this is where the game checks whether you've been upgrading smartly or spreading too thin. If you're stuck:
1. Make sure you've unlocked lasers
2. Check your sweep arc coverage — orcs should pass through at least two turret arcs before reaching your base
3. Farm 3.1 a few more times for crystals

**Level 6.1 is the second wall.** By this point you need all turret types at reasonable upgrade levels, layered defenses with multiple overlapping kill zones, and at least one panic button (airstrike or nuke) for emergencies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does it take to beat?**
The developers target 4 to 6 hours for a full clear of all 12 maps. Your mileage varies based on how well you optimize upgrades.

**Q: Is the game worth $9?**
With 86% positive reviews on Steam, 100K+ wishlists before launch, and a satisfying incremental loop, it's one of the best-value indie releases of summer 2026.

**Q: Does losing actually help?**
Yes — you keep all crystals from failed runs. The game is balanced around farming levels before beating them. If you're dying, you're not losing progress. You're banking upgrades.

**Q: What's the most common beginner mistake?**
Not unlocking new turret types before upgrading stats. A diversified loadout with one of each turret type beats a maxed-out flamethrower and nothing else every time.

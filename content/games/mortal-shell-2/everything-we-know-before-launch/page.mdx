Mortal Shell II arrives on **August 20, 2026**, and it's doing something no soulslike has dared to do: **remove the stamina bar entirely.** No green bar ticking down. No forced retreats mid-combo. No tapping R1 three times and then standing there gasping while a knight caves your skull in. Cold Symmetry is betting that players are ready for a more aggressive, fluid combat style — and after sinking 12 hours into the June open beta, I think they might be onto something.

Here's everything you need to know before launch day, from the eight playable Shells to the edition breakdown to whether you should actually pre-order this thing.

## What Is Mortal Shell II?

Mortal Shell II is a standalone sequel to the 2020 dark fantasy soulslike developed by Cold Symmetry and published by Playstack. You play as **the Harbinger**, an entity that can inhabit the bodies of fallen warriors called **Shells** — each with their own combat style, abilities, and backstory.

The first game was a compact, budget-priced soulslike that earned a cult following for its moody atmosphere and the unique "Hardening" mechanic (freeze mid-animation to become temporarily invincible). The sequel expands on every front: bigger world, more Shells, deeper combat, and a shift from linear levels to a compact open world packed with **60+ dungeons**.

If you enjoyed the oppressive atmospheres of [Elden Ring's best builds](/games/elden-ring/best-builds-tier-list-2025) or the methodical combat of [Black Myth: Wukong's build guides](/games/black-myth-wukong/best-builds-2026), Mortal Shell II is aiming squarely at the same dark-fantasy soulslike audience — but with a radically different combat philosophy.

## The Big Change: No Stamina Bar

This is the headline feature, and it's the thing I kept coming back to during the beta. Every soulslike from Dark Souls to Elden Ring to Lies of P has trained players to respect the stamina bar. Attack twice, dodge, back off, wait for it to refill. Mortal Shell II throws that entire rhythm out the window.

Without stamina gating your attacks, combat becomes about **positioning and timing** rather than resource management. You can swing as many times as you want — but so can enemies. The trade-off is that every attack has recovery frames, and getting greedy means eating a counterattack that could one-shot you from full health.

<ProTip title="Unlearn Your Soulslike Habits">
If you come from Elden Ring or Dark Souls, your instinct will be to attack 2-3 times and back off. In Mortal Shell II, that habit will get you killed. Enemies are designed to punish hit-and-run tactics. Commit to your combos, learn the recovery frames, and use the Hardening mechanic to absorb counterattacks mid-swing.
</ProTip>

I learned this the hard way. During the beta, I fought a tall, armored knight in a flooded cathedral and tried to play it safe — two hits, roll away, repeat. After three attempts of getting nowhere, I realized the knight was specifically punishing my retreats with a leaping slash that tracked my dodge. On attempt four, I went aggressive: four-hit combo into a Harden to absorb his counter, then a finisher. He went down in under 60 seconds. That moment completely reframed how I saw the game.

The [official Mortal Shell II Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2465920/Mortal_Shell_II/) describes the combat as "ruthless and fast," and that's accurate — but it's also more readable than the first game. Enemy telegraphs are cleaner, and the removal of stamina means you're always engaged, never waiting.

## The 8 Playable Shells

Shells are the heart of Mort Shell II's identity. Each Shell is the preserved body of a fallen warrior, and inhabiting them grants you their combat abilities, stats, and unique powers. The full game ships with **8 Shells** — here's what we know so far:

| Shell | Playstyle | Signature Ability | Source |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| **Harros, the Black Apostle** | Aggressive striker | Can turn enemies to stone temporarily | Open beta |
| **Tiel the Acolyte** | Fast, mobile, returning from MS1 | Enhanced dodge with invincibility frames | Open beta |
| **Eredrim the Venerable** | Heavy tank, high poise | Unknown (full game) | Revealed in previews |
| **Proxima the Broodseeker** | Unknown | Unknown | Revealed in previews |
| **Shell #5-8** | Unknown | Unknown | Launch day |

Harros was my go-to in the beta. His stone ability creates a window where you can wail on a tough enemy without worrying about their attacks — essentially a free combo pass. Tiel returns from the original game with his signature speed, and his enhanced dodge makes him the natural choice for players who prefer a hit-and-run style (even if the game discourages it).

The remaining four Shells are under wraps until launch, but Cold Symmetry has confirmed that each will have a fully voiced backstory you uncover by finding **lost memories** scattered throughout the world. These memories aren't just lore dumps — they unlock new abilities for each Shell, creating a progression system tied to exploration rather than XP grinding.

<InfoBox title="Shell Switching Is Free">
Unlike the first game, where swapping Shells required returning to specific statues, Mortal Shell II lets you switch between unlocked Shells freely. This means you can tailor your loadout to each boss or dungeon without backtracking.
</InfoBox>

## The World: Compact but Dense

The biggest structural change from the first game is the move to a **compact open world**. Instead of linear levels connected by hub areas, Mortal Shell II gives you a contiguous world to explore — ruined temples, frozen graveyards, ancient forests, and towering citadels.

Cold Symmetry has been clear that this isn't an Elden Ring-sized map. It's intentionally compact, with **60+ dungeons** layered into a space designed to be traversable on foot. The goal is density over scale — every direction you walk should lead to something worth finding within a minute or two.

Based on the beta, this design philosophy works. The starting area alone contained three dungeons (a flooded crypt, a cliffside temple, and a forest clearing with a mini-boss), all within a two-minute sprint of each other. None felt padded or reused — each had distinct architecture and enemy placements.

## Editions and Pricing

Mortal Shell II launches with three editions. Here's the breakdown:

| Edition | Price | Platforms | What's Included |
|---------|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Standard Edition** | $49.99 | PC, PS5, Xbox | Base game + Bone Harbinger pre-order skin |
| **Devout Edition** | $59.99 | PC, PS5, Xbox | Base game + 72h early access + Obsidian skins for all 8 Shells + Bone Harbinger skin |
| **Revered Edition** | TBA | PS5 (physical only) | SteelBook case, 100-page artbook, art prints, collector's box |

The pre-order bonus applies to all editions: the **Bone Harbinger** skin and the **Obsidian Harbinger** variant, which changes the Harbinger's visual appearance.

## Should You Pre-Order?

Here's where I have to be honest with you: **I recommend the Devout Edition for returning players, but new players should wait for reviews.**

If you played the original Mortal Shell and enjoyed it, the Devout Edition at $59.99 is a solid deal. The 72-hour early access means you're playing on August 17 instead of August 20, and the Obsidian skins for all 8 Shells are genuinely nice cosmetics that'll last the entire playthrough. The $10 premium over the Standard Edition is reasonable for what you get.

If you're new to the series or new to soulslikes in general, **wait for launch day reviews.** The no-stamina-bar system is a radical departure from genre norms, and while I loved it in the beta, it's not going to click for everyone. The first Mortal Shell was divisive — some players found it too short and too obtuse — and the sequel's combat changes could be equally polarizing. Drop $50 on August 21 after you've read a few reviews, not $60 on a pre-order based on hype.

<ProTip title="PS5 Physical Collectors: Watch for the Revered Edition">

</ProTip>

One more thing: the [open beta from June 2026](https://www.nichegamer.com/reviews/mortal-shell-2-preview/) is no longer available, but Cold Symmetry has indicated they're considering a second beta closer to launch. If you're on the fence, follow their social channels — a hands-on session will tell you more than any preview article can.

## The Bottom Line

Mortal Shell II is shaping up to be one of the most interesting soulslike releases of 2026. The decision to remove the stamina bar is the kind of bold mechanical change that could either redefine the genre or alienate purists — and based on my beta experience, I'm leaning toward the former. The combat feels fresh, the Shells system adds genuine build variety, and the compact open world design respects your time without sacrificing depth.

If you're a soulslike veteran looking for something that breaks the mold, August 20 can't come soon enough. If you're new to the genre, keep this on your radar and check back at launch for our full review.

Mortal Shell II is out **today, August 20, 2026**, on PC (Steam), PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X|S — and the review embargo lifted right alongside the Standard Edition unlock. The critical consensus: this is the best-reviewed game Cold Symmetry has ever shipped, sitting at **84 on Metacritic (PC) and 86 on OpenCritic** at the time of writing, against a 76 for the 2020 original.

But there's a wrinkle most roundups aren't making obvious: **the majority of those reviews were scored against the pre-launch build.** Cold Symmetry dropped a substantial day one patch on top of early access that rebalances the back half of the game, reworks several Shells and Tarstones, deletes Gloom farming spots, and fixes the exact camera and progression complaints critics flagged. Start fresh today and you're playing a meaningfully different game than the one reviewers scored.

The praise was near-universal: the no-stamina combat is faster, more aggressive, and "heavenly" after the original's molasses pace, in GamesRadar+'s words. Shells now feel like distinct character builds rather than different health bars, with free respecs encouraging experimentation. The complaints were just as consistent: **camera behavior in tight fights, difficulty spikes in the late game, a handful of progression blockers, and some bugs.** Which brings us to the part that changes your launch-day experience.

## How Long Is Mortal Shell II?

Cold Symmetry officially pegs the average playthrough at **30–40 hours**, with playthroughs seen breaking 60. Independent reporting lines up:

- **Critical path (story rush):** 20+ hours
- **Standard playthrough (main + optional dungeons):** 30–40 hours
- **100% completionist run:** 50–60+ hours (all 60+ dungeons, all Shell memories, full map)
- **All achievements/trophies:** ~60 hours (requires NG+ and NG++ runs)

That's roughly triple the original Mortal Shell's 10–12 hours — the sequel doubles down on scale without padding for its own sake.

## All 9 Shells, Confirmed

The full roster is now public: **nine Shells total, eight permanent plus one prologue-only body.** Harros, the returning starter from the first game, is yours for the opening only — you lose him permanently once the prologue ends. The eight you can keep:

- **Fainweald region:** Tiel (stealth/mobility), Proxima (defensive utility), Gragu (tavern quest reward), Eredrim (heavy stagger tank), Smert (high-risk Miracle caster)
- **Mammon region:** Lazlo, Sariel (parasitic thorns/counter build), Genessa (summons + second-chance survivability)

## Verdict: Buy Today?

**If you like soulslikes: yes.** At $49.99, a mid-80s Metacritic score with a genuinely different combat system — no stamina bar, posture-driven parries, firearms woven into melee — makes this the strongest non-FromSoft soulslike of 2026 so far, and the day one patch addressed most launch complaints before you ever touched the game.

**If you're new to the genre:** the game is fairer and more approachable than the original by design, but it's still a punishing soulslike. Commit $50 if you've beaten Elden Ring or Nioh; otherwise wait for a sale.

The one honest caveat: the patch doesn't erase the fact that the launch build needed fixing at all. Camera work in crowded fights and late-game balance are improved, not perfected. But a 9/10 post-patch re-review, a 91%-positive open beta, and a day one patch this substantive all point the same way: **Cold Symmetry shipped the real deal.**

## FAQ

### Do I need to have played Mortal Shell (2020) first?
No — it's a standalone sequel. You'll miss some lore callbacks and the emotional weight of Harros's prologue role, but nothing mechanically requires the original.

### Will my open beta progress carry over?
Only two things carry from the beta: The Flayed Harbinger cosmetic and, if you cleared Marrow Keep, a Prologue skip. All currency, weapons, Shells, and collectibles reset by design.

### When is the exact launch time?
Mortal Shell II unlocked worldwide **August 20 at 6:00 AM PT** on all three platforms — the Standard Edition is live now.

## The Bottom Line

Mortal Shell II is the rare sequel that both gets the scale right and fixes what held its predecessor back. Reviews are strong (84 Metacritic / 86 OpenCritic), the day one patch closed most of the gaps critics found, and the no-stamina combat delivers on its promise of a faster, more readable soulslike. If you've been waiting for the genre's 2026 answer to Elden Ring, it's here — and it costs less than most of the competition.

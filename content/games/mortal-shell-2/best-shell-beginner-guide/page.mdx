## First, Understand What Changed: No Stamina Bar

Every attack, dodge, and sprint in Mortal Shell 2 is **unrestricted by stamina**. You can swing indefinitely — and so can enemies. Combat is governed instead by recovery frames, positioning, and two signature systems:

- **Hardening** — freeze mid-animation to become briefly invincible. It's not an emergency panic button; it's an offensive tool. Start an attack, harden through the enemy's counter, finish your combo when the hit is absorbed.
- **Perfect Guard** — enemies telegraph unblockable attacks with a red flash. Guard at the exact moment and you stagger them into a riposte window with massive execution damage.

<ProTip title="Unlearn Your Souls Habits First">
The number one killer of Elden Ring players: attacking 2-3 times and retreating to "reset stamina" that doesn't exist. Enemies in Mortal Shell 2 are designed to punish retreat and hesitation. Commit to your strings, harden through the response, and stay inside combo range. Practice the harden timing on a repeatable trash mob — never learn it for the first time during a boss.
</ProTip>

## Which Shell Should You Start With?

**Tiel is your first and default answer.** He's the first body you claim after the prologue, and his kit rewards exactly what this game wants from you: aggression. Shadow Dash gives you a shadow state when you dodge with correct timing, and Shadow Strike lets you phase through enemies for devastating ambush hits. With no stamina tax on his rapid dual-wield strings, Tiel is the smoothest on-ramp for new players — and for soulslike veterans, he's the most fun expression of the no-stamina philosophy.

**Reach for Eredrim when the game starts hitting back.** The returning tank carries the biggest health pool and an absorb-focused playstyle that forgives mistimed hardens. If a boss is repeatedly one-shotting Tiel's thinner body, Eredrim is your wall — slower, heavier, but he takes hits that would eject you from a lighter Shell. In the original game he was the beginner crutch, and early reports suggest that identity survives.

**Proxima is the skilled player's toy.** Her left-arm hook pulls enemies — or herself — into lightning-inflicted follow-ups, and Grafted Armor's 15% chance to halve incoming damage and prevent stagger makes her uniquely suited to staying inside dangerous bosses. High skill ceiling, less immediately forgiving than the other two.

## The Systems That Will Kill You If You Ignore Them

**Gloom is your XP — spend it before you gamble it.** Enemies and Gloom trees drop Gloom, which you invest in Harbinger upgrades at Beacons. Die while carrying a full wallet and it drops at your death location, souls-style. Before entering any unfamiliar boss arena or dungeon, spend down.

**Beacons are your checkpoints.** Activate every single one you pass — they save, heal, upgrade, and anchor your recovery runs. A good early route chains a Beacon, a Shell or weapon objective, and a return path.

**Shell Swapping is free this time.** Unlike the original, you're not locked into trekking back to statues — you can adapt your body to the fight. At Marrow Keep, speak with **Zhirelle the Shellkeeper**, who runs the Bonding system: relive each Shell's memories, unlock lore, and purchase ability upgrades.

**Your ranged option is a shotgun pistol.** It consumes Resolve (the yellow meter you build by landing melee hits) rather than ammunition, interrupts enemy wind-ups at range, and shreds weakened elites. Full Resolve also fuels petrification bursts that freeze everything around you — a genuine panic button.

**Valheim** is leaving Early Access. After more than five and a half years, Iron Gate AB is pushing version **1.0 live on September 9, 2026**, and the update is far more than a version-number bump. The Deep North — the eighth and final biome — finally opens, the game launches on **PlayStation 5** and **Nintendo Switch 2** for the first time, and every platform gets full crossplay. If you already own Valheim, the update is free. If you are on the fence, the price has moved from its long-standing $19.99 Early Access tag to **$29.99** as of this writing.

I have been sailing Valheim's archipelago since the February 2021 Early Access launch, from the first terrified nights in the Meadows to the moment my group finally cleared the Ashlands. The 1.0 release feels like the inflection point the entire roadmap has been building toward, and the biggest practical question is not whether to play — it is how to get the cleanest start. This guide collects every confirmed detail from Iron Gate, Coffee Stain Publishing, and the official 1.0 FAQ, then turns those facts into an actual pre-launch plan.

## What Valheim 1.0 Actually Means

Version 1.0 is the official end of Early Access. The live default branch as of this writing is **0.221.12**, and Iron Gate has confirmed there is **no public test branch for 1.0** — everyone gets the build at the same time.

The headline additions are:

- **The Deep North biome** — the final region of the world, added to the northern edge of every seed.
- **New platforms** — PS5 and Nintendo Switch 2 join PC, Linux, Mac, Xbox One, and Xbox Series X|S.
- **Full crossplay** across all platforms on compatible servers.
- **50+ achievements** added for the first time.
- **A $19.99 → $29.99 price increase** for new purchases.

From a content standpoint, 1.0 also introduces new enemy types, three new armor tiers, a Nord weapon line, the Frostfire Crossbow, a grappling hook, new building pieces, new food recipes, and alternative biome variants such as the Rocky Birch Meadows and Deforested Black Forest. The game also receives a broader world-generation pass, performance optimizations, and an overhauled crafting menu.

I would not treat this like a routine content patch. In my view, the most important change is structural: Valheim now has a complete progression from Meadows to Deep North, and the studio has said the Deep North is the final biome. That makes 1.0 the definitive version to recommend to new players.

## Crossplay, Cross-Save, and Mods

Crossplay is one of the biggest quality-of-life changes in 1.0. Here is how it actually works based on Iron Gate's official 1.0 FAQ and press materials.

- **Full crossplay** is supported between PC, Xbox, PS5, and Nintendo Switch 2 on the same server.
- **Cross-save is limited** to Xbox consoles and the Microsoft Store version on PC. Steam, PS5, and Switch 2 saves do not sync.
- **Each platform is a separate purchase.** Buying on Steam does not unlock the PS5 version.
- **Server limit remains 1-10 players.**
- **PS Plus is required** for online multiplayer on PS5.
- **No split-screen or couch co-op.**

The crossplay system runs on Microsoft's PlayFab backend when enabled. That matters for PC players because enabling crossplay switches Valheim's networking layer away from the Steam-based setup and disables the **BepInEx mod framework**. If your group relies on quality-of-life or gameplay mods, you have a real choice to make: either disable crossplay and keep mods, or enable crossplay and run vanilla.

I am planning to keep our dedicated server vanilla for the first month so console friends can join, then spin up a second modded server for the long-term crew. Vanilla Valheim in 2026 is a much cleaner experience than it was in 2021 — the crafting menu overhaul and expanded inventory alone remove most of the friction that made mods feel mandatory — but inventory management mods are still hard to give up once you are used to them.

## Fresh World or Existing Save?

Iron Gate's official recommendation is to **start a fresh world** for 1.0. That is guidance, not a requirement.

The technical reality is:

- Existing characters, worlds, and saves remain compatible.
- New terrain and biome content can only generate in **unexplored areas**.
- The Deep North has always existed as an unfilled placeholder at the northern edge of every seed; 1.0 fills unexplored portions of that zone.
- If your group has already sailed the far north and revealed those map tiles, that specific territory will stay empty.

For achievement hunters, tracking begins only after 1.0 is installed. Accomplishments from older saves, such as defeating a boss, may need to be repeated before the related achievement unlocks. Cheats and developer commands will also now flag the affected character or world, and spawned items will be visibly marked.

My personal plan is to start a fresh world with the same crew. Valheim's progression is tuned around the Meadows → Black Forest → Swamp → Mountain → Plains → Mistlands → Ashlands → Deep North arc, and jumping straight into the Deep North on a maxed character skips the pacing that makes the new gear and recipes meaningful. If you have a beautiful long-term build you do not want to abandon, keep it — but consider creating a second world specifically for the 1.0 experience.

## Deep North Progression: Gear, Food, and Build Order

Iron Gate has confirmed that **Mistlands-tier gear is the practical floor** for the Deep North. You do not need a fully maxed Ashlands loadout, but showing up in Plains armor is asking to be one-shot by a Gammeltroll.

Here is the progression loop I expect to follow during the first week:

1. **Establish a forward base** near the Deep North coast. Use the new shovel to clear deep snow before you place the workbench and portal.
2. **Farm Ember Charges early.** Gammeltrolls are not optional farming targets — they are the gate to the new armor and weapon materials. Without Ember Charges, their corpses turn to stone and drop nothing.
3. **Build the Frost Foundry.** This workshop station needs room, so plan a longhouse expansion around it. It is used with moulds to cast Bloodgold into Nord weapons and Intricate Keys.
4. **Unlock the grappling hook.** It is located in the Mistlands progression chain after the Queen and the Galdr Table. Every character in your group needs this independently, because the Deep North's icy mesas are built around vertical traversal.
5. **Cook meatballs with lingonberries.** The new food line supports the higher health and stamina demands of the biome. Keep your old top-tier meals handy while you gather the new ingredients.

The Deep North is not just a harder version of the Ashlands. The snow, ice, and verticality change how you move and build. In the Ashlands, the challenge was mostly combat pressure and resource scarcity. In the Deep North, the environment itself is an opponent: slippery ice can send you sliding off a mesa, deep snow can block a quick portal retreat, and the snowstorms can hide enemies until you are already surrounded.

## What Is Not in 1.0

It is just as important to know the boundaries. Iron Gate has confirmed:

- **The Ocean biome is not getting a content update** in 1.0.
- **The Deep North is the final biome.** No ninth biome is planned.
- **Post-1.0 support** is currently described as bug fixes and quality-of-life; future expansions are not promised.
- **No universal cloud saves** beyond the Xbox / Microsoft Store PC link.
- **No cross-buy** between platforms.

If you are hoping for a second major expansion after 1.0, the studio has not committed to one. Treat 1.0 as the complete package.

## Should You Buy or Reinstall Valheim 1.0?

If you already own Valheim, the answer is simple: update and play. The 1.0 patch is free, and the Deep North gives the game a proper conclusion.

For new players, $29.99 is a fair asking price. Valheim offers dozens to hundreds of hours of co-op survival crafting, and 1.0 is the cleanest entry point. If you enjoy games like [Palworld](/games/palworld/complete-guide-1-0) or [Project Zomboid](/games/project-zomboid/build-42-survival-guide), Valheim sits in the same broad space — open-world survival with deep crafting, base building, and boss-driven progression — but with a distinct Viking-Norse aesthetic and a slower, more deliberate pace.

My recommendation is to buy it if you have a group to play with. Valheim is technically soloable, but the real magic is the shared saga: building a longhall, sailing through a storm, and losing your mind when a troll smashes the whole thing. If you are strictly single-player, the game is still excellent, but you should expect a more methodical grind.

For returning players who drifted away after the Ashlands, 1.0 is the best reason to reinstall since the original Early Access launch. The crafting menu overhaul, expanded inventory, and performance optimizations make the early game less clunky, while the Deep North gives endgame groups a clear final goal. Even if you only put in another twenty hours, you will see the game at its most complete.

The Blood of Dawnwalker review embargo lifted on **August 31**, and the full picture is finally in: **83 on Metacritic** across 41 critic reviews (93% positive, 0% negative), 84 on OpenCritic, and one of the most genuinely divided critical receptions of 2026. Rebel Wolves' debut — built by The Witcher 3 director Konrad Tomaszkiewicz and a team of CD Projekt Red veterans — launches **this Thursday, September 3** on PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S.

When we published our embargo-watch roundup on Monday, only one early score existed (InGameNews' 9/10). Now that every major outlet has weighed in, the story is more interesting than a single number: critics largely agree the writing and time-as-currency structure are special, but they are sharply split on combat depth, technical polish, and whether the 30-day clock holds up across a 50-plus hour playthrough. Here is every score that matters and what it tells you before you buy.

## What Critics Agree On

The consensus is unusually strong on two things. First, the **writing and worldbuilding** — every review, including the harsh ones, praises Vale Sangora's folkloric dark fantasy and the quality of the storytelling. Second, the **30-day clock**: treating time as a currency you spend on quests is the game's defining idea, and even skeptical critics credit it. IGN called the "wonderfully stressful" tradeoffs the best part of the adventure; GameSpot noted that the fact you *can't* do everything "is part of what makes it so compelling." The day/night dual-state — human by day, superpowered vampire by night — earned near-universal praise, with Eurogamer singling out night-time rooftop traversal as a highlight.

## What Splits Critics

The fault lines we flagged in our [preview roundup](/games/blood-of-dawnwalker/preview-roundup-2026s-breakout-rpg) turned out to be exactly where reviews diverge:

- **Combat longevity.** Game Informer called encounters "repetitive Simon Says sequences" with enemies that recycle movesets; Game Rant found the loop formulaic. Positive reviews see a real learning curve that "gets under your skin" (IGN) — your tolerance for repetition is the biggest swing factor.
- **Technical polish.** GameSpot and Multiplayer.it (8/10) both flagged shaky performance and visual glitches at launch, with Multiplayer.it calling the execution "far from flawless." A day-one patch is expected to address 60fps modes on PS5 and Xbox Series X — but not Series S, which reportedly stays at 30fps.
- **Pacing.** GamesRadar's 3/5 review said the quest structure "constantly stops me from getting immersed." This is a game that asks for a 50-70 hour commitment to feel its full structure.

## The Bottom Line

An 83 Metacritic with 93% positive reviews says this is a very good RPG that a large majority of critics recommend — but the shape of the scores (Vice 10/10 next to Game Rant 6/10) says it is a *specific* kind of RPG. If you're here for story, atmosphere, and hard choices, The Blood of Dawnwalker is the closest thing to a The Witcher 3 successor in a decade. If you're combat-first or sensitive to jank, give it a patch cycle. Either way, it has already earned its place in the September 2026 conversation — and with [The Witcher 3 Remastered](/games/witcher-3) landing September 29, this month is a brutal one for your backlog.

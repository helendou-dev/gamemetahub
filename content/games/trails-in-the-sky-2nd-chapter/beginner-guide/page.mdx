## Before You Start: Five Day-One Settings

The remaster defaults are mostly fine, but five settings are worth changing before you leave Estelle's room:

1. Turn on autosave and set it to every area transition. 2nd Chapter has long dungeons, and a single boss can wipe a careless party.
2. Set text advance to a single button press rather than auto-advance. Auto-advance races through voiced scenes and makes it easy to miss quest markers.
3. Enable combat turbo if you want faster random encounters. It does not affect boss fight balance, only animation speed.
4. Switch the minimap to always visible. Liberl is larger than the first game, and hidden paths are everywhere.
5. Set difficulty to Normal unless you have already finished the first chapter on Hard or Nightmare. 2nd Chapter's early bosses punish under-leveled parties, and the orbment system matters more than raw stats.

## What 2nd Chapter Actually Adds

If you are coming straight from Trails in the Sky FC, the sequel raises the stakes in three ways:

- The world map opens up faster. You are not locked to a single region per chapter for as long.
- Chain crafts and combination attacks become more common, so party synergy matters more than individual damage numbers.
- The Ouroboros threat pushes the plot forward, which means missable side quests are tighter and some reward unique quartz.

For newcomers who skipped FC, the remaster includes a short story recap from the main menu, but I do not recommend relying on it. The emotional beats land because you know the cast. If you are on the fence, the first chapter is the better starting point, even though 2nd Chapter is the stronger game.

## Orbment and Quartz: The Real Combat Layer

Combat in Trails in the Sky looks like a standard turn-based grid system, but the real depth lives in the orbment menu. Each character equips quartz into their orbment slots, and the combination of quartz determines which Arts they can cast and which passive bonuses they receive.

## Combat Timing: AT Bonuses and S-Crafts

The action timeline on the left side of the screen is not just turn order. Some turns have glowing bonuses, such as increased critical rate, HP recovery, or instant casting. The AI will try to manipulate these, and so should you.

- Delaying enemies with Time Arts or crafts pushes their turns back and can deny them a bonus turn.
- Saving your S-Craft for a bonus-crit turn turns a boss fight around, especially when the enemy is about to use a big Art.
- Casting Arts takes time and can be interrupted. Against fast enemies, use crafts or instant spells, or protect the caster with a front-line character.

CP builds through taking damage and landing attacks. Do not blow S-Crafts on trash mobs unless you are about to hit a save point and want to spend excess CP before it caps.

## Cooking, Shops, and Money

Cooking is not just flavor. Recipes restore HP, EP, and CP, remove status effects, and sometimes grant stat buffs for the next fight. More importantly, cooked food is cheaper than buying the same recovery items outright.

Your priorities in each town:

1. Check the weapon shop for orbment upgrades. Better orbment slots unlock stronger quartz combinations.
2. Buy every recipe from the inn or food stall. Recipes are often missable if you leave a region too early.
3. Stock up on status-healing items before long dungeons. Sleep and confuse are especially punishing.
4. Sell old quartz you are not using. Sepith is far more valuable than the small amount of mira you get back, but every little bit helps early on.

<InfoBox>Do not hoard consumables. Trails in the Sky expects you to use food and items in boss fights. A boss that takes twenty minutes because you are saving your best items is not good resource management.</InfoBox>

## Equipment, Sepith, and the Mid-Game Orbment

Weapons and armor in 2nd Chapter are straightforward: each town sells a better version, and chests in dungeons sometimes hand you a free upgrade a chapter early. The rule is simple: if the weapon shop has a green arrow next to a character's slot, buy it. Armor is more situational. When a boss is coming up, prioritize pieces with status resistance over raw defense. Sleep, confuse, and seal can end a fight faster than raw damage.

Sepith is the real bottleneck. You need it to buy quartz, upgrade orbment slots, and occasionally pay for story-related fees. There are three reliable ways to keep your sepith income healthy:

1. Fight every new enemy type at least once. The bracer notebook rewards bestiary completion with sepith and sometimes items.
2. Break every wooden box and barrel in towns and dungeons. They respawn when you leave an area, and the contents add up.
3. Sell old equipment instead of hoarding it. A level 10 weapon is not coming back into rotation.

By the mid-game, your orbment boards will have more slots and higher-level sockets. That is when quartz combinations become powerful. A single high-tier quartz can unlock an Art that carries you through multiple chapters, while a well-balanced set of passive quartz can turn a fragile mage into a fast artillery piece.

## Missables and the Bracer Notebook

This is the single most important habit for a clean first playthrough. The bracer notebook tracks side quests, deadlines, and the hidden report book entries. Many side quests expire at the end of a chapter or when you travel to a new region.

The rules are simple:

- Check the notebook every time you enter a new town.
- Complete side quests before starting the next story objective, unless the quest explicitly says it carries over.
- Talk to NPCs twice. Many quests only appear after you have spoken to someone once and then reloaded the area.
- Read the in-game books. Some entries are tied to achievements and one-time chests.

The most valuable missables are recipes and unique quartz. Both can make the mid-game much easier. If you miss a few, it is not run-ending, but you will feel the gap during the harder boss fights.

## Difficulty and When to Grind

Normal difficulty is well-tuned for a first run. You should not need to grind random encounters unless you skip most side content. If a boss is consistently wiping you, the usual culprit is orbment setup, not levels.

Before retrying a boss, try this checklist:

1. Update weapons and armor in the last town.
2. Rebalance quartz for the encounter. Bosses that cast a lot need Water/Mirage resistance; physical bruisers need higher defense and Earth quartz.
3. Cook stat-buffing food before the fight.
4. Stock status heals and revival items.
5. Look at the AT bar. If the boss keeps taking bonus turns, add a Time caster to delay it.

Hard and Nightmare modes are best saved for a second run, when you understand orbment synergy and have a sense of which bosses demand specific setups.

## Audio, Localization, and Visual Options

The remaster ships with dual audio: English and Japanese voiceover. You can switch at any time from the settings menu. The English dub is strong, but some players prefer the original Japanese for the established cast.

Visual options include resolution scaling on PC, UI size adjustments for handheld Switch play, and the choice between classic sprite filtering and the cleaner remastered look. On Switch 2, the game targets 1080p docked and 720p handheld at a stable 60 frames per second. PlayStation 5 and PC support 4K output.

## What to Do in Your First Two Hours

Here is a condensed opening checklist if you just want to know what matters right now:

1. Watch or read the story recap if you did not finish FC recently.
2. Set the options listed above.
3. Talk to everyone in the starting town and accept every notebook entry.
4. Build Estelle as a balanced fighter with one Water quartz for an emergency heal.
5. Give your first mage Wind and Water quartz to unlock early offensive and healing Arts.
6. Do not sell sepith. Save it for the orbment shop in the first major town.
7. Cook your first recipe as soon as you find one.
8. Save before every boss and every multi-floor dungeon entrance.

If you follow that loop, the first chapter will feel manageable even if the story throws a few curveballs.

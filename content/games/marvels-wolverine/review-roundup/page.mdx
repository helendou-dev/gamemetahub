## What Critics Agree On

Reading the previews side by side, a clear consensus emerges — and it's more specific than "previews were positive."

**The combat feel is the strength.** Every single account praises the moment-to-moment violence: the weight of the claws, the destructible environments, and the three-tier Rage system that rewards staying aggressive. GameSpot and Eurogamer both describe combat that escalates into a genuine rampage, and the parry-to-berserker-frenzy loop is repeatedly called satisfying.

**The presentation is Insomniac at full budget.** Gore, dismemberment, finishing animations, and a ray-traced 60fps Performance Mode set as the default. The story content shown — Logan on a Team X mission with Mystique and Sabretooth, hunting Bolivar Trask — was described as dark and tonally distinct from Spider-Man.

**The performances are landing.** Liam McIntyre's Wolverine, Troy Baker as Nathaniel Essex, and the supporting cast drew some of the strongest praise in the entire preview cycle. Skill Up called the storytelling the single most impressive element.

## The One Big Question Mark

The consistent reservation — from IGN, Eurogamer, and Skill Up alike — is **variety**. Logan's toolkit is narrower than Spider-Man's: claws, parries, and forward momentum rather than gadgets and aerial improvisation. IGN's preview put it bluntly: "Wolverine mostly just angrily stabs things... I did wish that what he does offered a bit more variety."

## What Score Should You Expect?

Insomniac's recent track record sets the bar. Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (2023) landed at 90 on Metacritic, the original Marvel's Spider-Man (2018) at 87, and Spider-Man: Miles Morales at 85. Sony's willingness to lift the embargo five days early is the strongest signal yet that the studio expects to clear it.

Our own read from the preview cycle: the consensus praise for combat feel and presentation, set against a fair critique of variety, points to a landing zone in the **mid-to-high 80s** — excellent, if short of the genre-defining 90+ tier. The swing factor is the story: previews only saw the opening, and the writers (led by Walt Williams of Spec Ops: The Line) are clearly holding the best material back.

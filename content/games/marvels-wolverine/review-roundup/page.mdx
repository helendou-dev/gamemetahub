## What Critics Agree On

The post-embargo picture is more divided than the previews suggested. Here's what the full reviews surface:

**The combat feel is genuine — at first.** Every reviewer from IGN to Hardcore Gamer praises the moment-to-moment violence: the weight of the claws, the parry-to-berserker loop, and the gore that doesn't hide behind comic flashes. TheGamer, Push Square, and DualShockers call it satisfying from start to finish. The knocks — from IGN, Eurogamer, Giant Bomb, and GamesRadar+ alike — are that the toolkit (claws, parries, rage) wears thin across a 15–20 hour campaign, and the level design doesn't give it room to evolve.

**The presentation is Insomniac at full visual budget.** Multiple outlets single out PS5 Pro face models, ray tracing, and the cast — Liam McIntyre as Logan, Troy Baker as Nathaniel Essex (a.k.a. Mister Sinister), and Jean Grey draw consistent praise. Where Insomniac flexed with Spider-Man's Manhattan, here the spectacle sits in narrower corridors and set-pieces.

**The scores split on story and scope.** Mainstream critics (IGN, GameSpot, GamesRadar+, Eurogamer, VGC, Polygon, Kotaku) landed in the 5–6/10 range, citing a thinner, more linear structure than Spider-Man and a story that doesn't pay off the way previews suggested. Enthusiast and international sites (Hardcore Gamer, COGconnected, DualShockers, Game Informer, TheGamer) carried the average into the 8–10 range, focusing on character work and emotional storytelling.

## The One Big Question Mark

The strongest reservation across both preview and review cycles — from IGN, Eurogamer, GamesRadar+, Giant Bomb, and Skill Up alike — is **player agency and variety**. Logan's toolkit is narrower than Spider-Man's: claws, parries, and forward momentum rather than gadgets and aerial improvisation. Eurogamer's Chris Tapsell put it bluntly: "the lack of agency you have as a player, the lack of expressive, inventive, melodramatic or just silly options" is the real issue, comparing it unfavorably to the leverage you got from even a manhole cover in Spider-Man.

<ProTip title="What to Watch on September 15">
The score plateaued in the high 70s because critics couldn't reconcile Insomniac's brute-force action strengths with Wolverine's narrower kit. If you value emotional storytelling and a tight 15–20 hour campaign over gadget variety, the 8+ outlets (Push Square, TheGamer, DualShockers, Game Informer) are closer to your taste. If you want Spider-Man-level traversal depth and open-world breadth, the mainstream 6/10 reviews are an honest signal to wait.
</ProTip>

## The Reviews Are In: 78 Metacritic Score

The headline numbers across both major aggregators:

- **Metacritic: 78/100** based on 110 critic reviews ("Generally Favorable")
- **OpenCritic: 80/100** based on 59 critics, 75% Must Play / "Strong"
- 0 negative reviews on Metacritic; the floor is Giant Bomb's 50, the ceiling is Hardcore Gamer's 100
- Roughly 30 reviews landed at 80, ~14 at 90+, with 5 perfect scores (COGconnected, Hardcore Gamer, ScreenHub, GamingBolt, Impulsegamer)

The mainstream-critical average is closer to **64/100** — IGN, GameSpot, GamesRadar+, Eurogamer, VGC, The Guardian, Gamekult, and ComicBook all landed at 60 — while enthusiast and international outlets pulled the overall average into the high 70s.

Insomniac's recent track record puts the 78 in sharp relief. Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (2023) landed at 90 on Metacritic, the original Marvel's Spider-Man (2018) at 87, and Spider-Man: Miles Morales at 85. Wolverine slots in at **78**, the studio's lowest-scoring Marvel superhero release — well short of the genre-defining 90+ tier its Spider-Man games set.

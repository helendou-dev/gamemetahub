## Where Critics Agree

Across the board — even at the low end of the scale — reviewers converge on the same core strengths.

**The atmosphere is the star.** Multiple outlets call the flooded, Lovecraft-drenched Arkham itself the most frightening element in the game. Wccftech wrote that smaller audio cues — ice crunching underfoot, buzzing flies, sloshing water — sell the dread as effectively as any set piece. We felt the same during our time with the demo: the shift from detective adventure to survival horror is Frogwares' best creative decision in years.

**Puzzles remain the backbone.** GameSpew singled out the puzzle design as one of the strongest elements of the game, with many conundrums entirely optional and tied to exploration. That's a welcome holdover from the first game's investigation DNA.

**Combat is weightier than expected.** Multiple reviews describe encounters as challenging and "weighty," noting that running is sometimes the correct answer — classic survival horror tension. The dodge mechanic and boil-bursting weak points on enemies keep early fights from feeling like shooting galleries.

## Where They Clash

The disagreement is real, and it mostly comes down to expectations versus execution.

**The investigation pivot.** The original's open-world detective structure has been streamlined into a more linear, story-focused campaign. Critics who wanted more of the first game's freedom (Wccftech, Push Square) flag this as the main loss; critics who wanted a tighter experience (Player 2, GameSpew) call it the game's biggest win. If you loved the first game's open world, set expectations accordingly.

**Boss fights and pacing.** GamesRadar+ and CGMagazine both note that challenge segments and boss encounters land unevenly, and that some areas don't flow well into each other — the final hours apparently drag for some reviewers while feeling perfectly paced for others.

**Technical polish.** This is the biggest red flag in the room. Frogwares admitted before launch that the game ships with known issues: stuttering, visual bugs, slow texture streaming, DLSS problems in performance mode, and frozen enemies. GameSpew on PS5 Pro reported a clean run in 60fps performance mode, but PC reviewers warn that optimization is a lottery right now.

## Should You Buy It Today?

Here's our honest read. At **$49.99 (Standard) / $54.99 (Deluxe) / $59.99 (Premium)**, the game is priced below the AAA survival horror tier, and most critics land around 13–15 hours for a full playthrough with New Game Plus and speedrun modes on top. The value case is strong if you're a survival horror fan.

- **Buy it today if** you're on PS5 or Xbox and love Resident Evil-style survival horror — the console reviews are more consistent, and GameSpew's clean PS5 Pro run is encouraging.
- **Wait a week if** you're on PC with a mid-range card — the known stuttering and DLSS issues are exactly the kind of thing the first post-launch patch typically fixes.

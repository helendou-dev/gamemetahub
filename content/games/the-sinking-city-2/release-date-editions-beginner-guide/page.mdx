## Which Edition Should You Buy?

For most players, **Standard at $49.99 is the smart pick**. Here's the reasoning:

- The Chthonic Arsenal — the only content with real gameplay value at the low tier — comes free with any pre-order.
- The Deluxe Field Kit is mostly cosmetic (outfits) plus consumable starter resources that a survival horror arguably wants you to scavenge yourself.
- Premium's $10 premium over Standard buys a 24-hour head start and a two-hour side mission. If you're a hardcore Frogwares fan or content creator racing the embargo, that's worth it. For everyone else, it's optional.

There are also two **5%-off bundles** on Steam: one pairing the game with The Sinking City Remastered, another with Sherlock Holmes The Awakened — solid entry points if you missed the studio's earlier work.

## Do You Need to Play The Sinking City First?

**No.** The Sinking City 2 is a fresh case with a new protagonist — **Calvin Rafferty**, replacing the first game's Charles Reed — in a new city (Arkham, not Oakmont), built around a failed dreaming ritual and the woman he's trying to save. The investigative DNA carries over, but the story stands alone. Returning players will catch Lovecraftian references; new players lose nothing essential.

## What Kind of Game Is It?

A third-person survival horror with deliberate, resource-lean pacing:

- **Scarcity by design** — ammo and healing are limited; retreating to rearm is a valid strategy, not a failure.
- **Arkham as a character** — a flooded Roaring '20s port city explored on foot and by skiff, with shortcuts behind collapsed seawalls and secrets in boarded-up parlors.
- **Investigations that don't gate you** — optional cases unlock safer routes, supplies, and weapon upgrades, but never block the main story.
- **A bestiary of mythos horrors** — the Slither animate the dead, Deep Ones surge from dark water, and Shadows bend space around you.

## Five Beginner Tips for Your First Hours in Arkham

1. **Fight less than you think.** This is not the first game's combat bolted onto detective work — encounters are designed to be survived, not cleared. Kiting and retreating through unlocked shortcuts is core loop, not cowardice.
2. **Open every shortcut before you need it.** Backtracking through safe routes for supplies and upgrades is how the game wants you to play. A locked shortcut behind you is a lifeline.
3. **Do optional investigations early.** They never block the story, but they hand out safer routes, resources, and gun upgrades that dramatically ease the mid-game difficulty curve.
4. **Save ammo for fights you can't avoid.** Between the Slither, Deep Ones, and Shadows, some enemies must be met head-on — the trick is knowing which. Melee and evasion carry you further than you'd expect.
5. **Play the Steam demo's opening hour first.** It teaches the resource rhythm with zero risk to your launch-day save file.

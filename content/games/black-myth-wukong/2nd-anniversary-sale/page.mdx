I had preloaded the game on Steam, woke up at 6 AM to beat the server rush, and proceeded to get absolutely demolished by the Wandering Wight in Chapter 1 for a solid hour. The game sold 4.5 million copies in its first 24 hours and crossed [10 million units within three days](https://dy.163.com/article/L3JCMGKT05118EDB.html). Two years later, it's sitting at an estimated 25+ million copies and still holds the crown as the best-selling Chinese-developed game of all time.

If you've been waiting for the "right price" — this is it.

## Is Black Myth: Wukong Still Worth Playing in 2026?

This is the question I get most from friends who skipped the launch hype. The short answer: **yes, but know what you're signing up for.**

### What Aged Well

The boss fights remain the undisputed highlight. Black Myth: Wukong is essentially a boss rush game disguised as an action RPG — over 80 unique boss encounters, each with distinct attack patterns, lore, and visual design. I replayed the first three chapters last month and the Yellow Wind Sage fight still gave me chills. The art direction, powered by Unreal Engine 5, has aged gracefully — the [ancient temple ruins in Chapter 2](https://m.zol.com.cn/article/12271188.html) still look better than most 2026 releases.

The Chinese mythology setting is genuinely unique in the AAA space. Most players I've talked to who bounced off Souls games found Wukong more accessible — no corpse runs, no stamina management, just a straightforward dodge-and-punish combat loop with diverse stances and spells.

### What Didn't Age as Well

The level design is... inconsistent. Some chapters (2 and 4) are dense and atmospheric, while others (6 especially) feel like rushed open zones with invisible walls everywhere. I got lost in Chapter 3's pagoda realm for a genuinely frustrating 40 minutes on my first playthrough, and revisiting it two years later, it hasn't gotten any better.

The story assumes you've read *Journey to the West*. If you haven't, prepare for a lot of "who is this person and why should I care" moments. The [ending is famously controversial](https://dy.163.com/article/L3JCMGKT05118EDB.html) — I won't spoil it, but let's just say I understood why it divided the community.

## Who Should Buy at ¥187.6?

**Buy it now if:**
- You enjoy boss-centric action games (Sekiro, Nioh, Wo Long)
- Chinese mythology and aesthetic appeal to you — there's nothing else like it at this scale
- You have a decent GPU. The game is optimized for modern hardware; I ran it on an RTX 3070 at 1440p with DLSS and had a smooth 60+ FPS throughout

**Skip or wait if:**
- You prefer open-world exploration with meaningful side content (Wukong is linear with hidden areas)
- You need a clear, hand-holding narrative — the story is fragmentary and demands cultural context
- Your PC is aging. The minimum spec is genuinely the *minimum* — I tried running it on a GTX 1660 Super for testing and Chapter 3's snow effects dropped it to sub-30 FPS

## The Bigger Picture: Wukong's Legacy at Two Years

Two years ago, *Black Myth: Wukong* wasn't just a game launch — it was the moment the global industry stopped treating Chinese AAA as a hypothetical. We're now seeing the ripple effects: Game Science's next title *Black Myth: Zhong Kui* is in development, and the broader wave of Chinese single-player games (like *Phantom Blade Zero* and *Where Winds Meet*) owe part of their momentum to Wukong proving the market exists.

I've been covering Chinese games for years, and the shift in conversation from "can China make a real AAA game?" to "which Chinese AAA are you most excited for?" happened almost entirely because of this one title. That's not hyperbole — that's what 25 million copies and a Best Action Game trophy at The Game Awards buys you.

The ¥187.6 price tag is, in context, an absurd value proposition. You're getting roughly 35-50 hours of main content (more if you chase the true ending) from a game that set the standard for an entire genre in its region. I paid full price on launch day and don't regret it. At 30% off, it's a no-brainer — if the game is your kind of thing.

Just make sure it actually is.

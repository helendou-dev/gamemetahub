**Game Science has finally shown Black Myth: Zhong Kui in motion.** A 15-minute gameplay reveal dropped on August 20 — exactly one year after the sequel was announced at [Gamescom 2025 Opening Night Live](https://www.gamescom.global/en) — and it's the first real look at how the studio's follow-up to Wukong actually plays. Captured from a work-in-progress build in 21:9, the footage trended at #1 on Bilibili within hours and cleared 3 million views there before the day was out, with fans calling the UE5 visuals "Netflix-level" in the comments.

If you need the full story so far — platforms, mythology, and release-date speculation — our [Black Myth: Zhong Kui everything we know guide](/games/black-myth-zhong-kui/everything-we-know) has the background. Here's what the new footage actually shows.

## This is not your Monkey King's combat

The biggest takeaway is that Zhong Kui plays nothing like Wukong. Gone are the staff stances and aerial acrobatics — the new protagonist fights with a **straight sword and bare-handed martial arts**, favoring grounded, deliberate strikes with a lot of weapon-to-weapon clashing. The [official gameplay page](https://blackmyth-zhongkui.com/en/gameplay/) breaks down the toolkit: sealing arts for capturing demons, soul transfiguration that alters your abilities, and spirit sight for spotting hidden paths.

Three mechanics stood out in the footage:

- **Parrying is the baseline.** Wukong required the Rock Solid skill to parry; Zhong Kui's protagonist deflects by default, and a stance bar fills as you hit, parry, and dodge to unlock sword techniques — closer to Phantom Blade Zero than to a Wukong reskin.
- **Elemental blades on demand.** Mid-fight, the hero flings a firebomb, ignites a cluster of enemies, then channels that fire into his sword for bonus damage. Later he charges the blade with lightning the same way.
- **A spirit partner who fights on his own.** A hulking warrior spirit fights alongside the protagonist and can be possessed directly for burst damage — the "tag-team" mechanic Game Science says is still experimental.

## A world with room to breathe

Wukong's biggest criticism was that the land between bosses felt empty. The Zhong Kui footage shows a denser world by design: the hero and an elderly companion build a raft that's smashed by a biblical-scale tidal wave, he dives to a lakebed to rescue a woman trapped by a clam-like monstrosity, and the montage closes with fishing, cooking, playing music with a skeleton trio, and even getting his makeup done. One Bilibili commenter noted the game is "moving beyond Wukong's dark aesthetic" toward something with more everyday life in it.

## The boss everyone's talking about

Roughly halfway through the video, the protagonist faces a **moth-like humanoid with shell-covered wings, barnacles, and scales** — a multi-phase fight that leans on the spirit partner, with the player taking direct control for the finishing blow. PC Gamer's [hands-on write-up](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/black-myth-zhong-kuis-15-minute-gameplay-reveal-shows-off-confident-swordplay-boss-fights-with-oyster-men-and-the-coolest-skeletons-ive-ever-seen/) called it "confident swordplay" and "the coolest skeletons I've ever seen."

## When does it come out?

Still no release date — Game Science reiterated the project "is still in early development" and that some mechanics shown are experimental. Platforms are confirmed as **PC and major consoles**, and the studio has stressed it won't rush the project or use AI in development. Don't expect it before 2028 at the earliest; Wukong took about four years between first gameplay and launch, and Zhong Kui appears to be on a similar arc.

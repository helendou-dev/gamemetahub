Here's whether Ghost Recon Wildlands is actually worth your time — and your $2.49 — in 2026.

## What changed: the Last Rites update

Ubisoft released the **free "Last Rites" update on August 6** as part of the Ghost Recon 25th anniversary celebration. It's not a minor patch — this is a substantial content drop:

- **New story mission**: "Last Rites" introduces Los Penitentes, a fanatical cult led by the antagonist La Llorona. New documents and audio logs flesh out the narrative with a darker tone than the base game.
- **4K / 60 FPS on consoles**: PS5, PS5 Pro, and Xbox Series X|S finally get current-gen performance. PC already supported high resolutions and frame rates, so you benefit from the new content directly.
- **Ghost Parameters**: Toggle instant death, unlimited revives, single or dual primary weapons, ammo retention on reload — essentially, dial the realism up or down to your taste.
- **Predator crossover returns**: One of Wildlands' most memorable events is back.
- **Free unlocks**: All Ghost War PvP classes, plus previously locked camouflage, weapons, and challenge rewards.

The update dropped alongside a free-to-play weekend that's now wrapped up, but the Steam discount runs through **August 16** — that's five more days at the time of writing.

## Does the gameplay still hold up?

Yes — and in some ways, it plays better than its 2019 sequel, Ghost Recon Breakpoint.

Wildlands was built around player-driven tactics rather than scripted set-pieces. Every cartel base, checkpoint, and convoy is a puzzle you can solve a dozen different ways: drone recon from the ridgeline, sync shot with your AI squad, full stealth infiltration, or go loud with mortars and helicopters. The map — a fictionalized Bolivia spanning salt flats, jungles, mountains, and towns — is one of the most varied open worlds in the genre.

The key mode to try: **realism mode**. Turn off the HUD, jack up the difficulty, and commit to every operation with no safety net. You'll be reading terrain, counting patrols, and coordinating shots like an actual operator. It transforms Wildlands from a casual shooter into one of the most rewarding solo tactical experiences available.

The AI isn't perfect — enemies occasionally forget where you are — and the main story is forgettable. But the core loop of planning, executing, and adapting on the fly still works beautifully. If you enjoy methodical, co-op open worlds, [Big Walk's recent launch](/games/big-walk/beginners-guide) explores a similar ethos in a completely different style.

## The catch

Three honest caveats:

1. **The story won't grab you.** You're here for the missions, not the narrative. The villain El Sueño is serviceable but not memorable.
2. **It can get repetitive.** If you grind objectives back-to-back instead of treating each operation as a standalone puzzle, the game's scale works against you. Play it slowly.
3. **Co-op is where it shines.** Solo is excellent, but the experience peaks with three friends coordinating simultaneous entries on a heavily guarded compound.

## Verdict: buy or skip?

**Buy.** At $2.49, this isn't a purchase — it's an impulse. The Last Rites update proves Ubisoft hasn't forgotten about Wildlands, the player surge means co-op lobbies are full, and the game itself remains one of the best tactical sandboxes on PC and console. If you've never played it, now is the best time in nine years. If you left after launch in 2017 and never came back, the game you remember is not the game that exists today.

The only reason to skip: you know you don't enjoy open-world military shooters. Otherwise, grab it before August 16 and turn the HUD off.

## Preload Status: Go Download Now

**Preloads are live as of today (August 2)** on Steam and console storefronts. If you've pre-ordered or redeemed a key, you can start downloading now.

The PC version clocks in at roughly **45 GB** and requires an SSD — no HDD fallback here. Console installs are lighter: around **30 GB on PS5** and **33 GB on Xbox Series X|S**. Budget for a day-one patch on top of those numbers.

If you're on Game Pass, the preload should also be available through the Xbox app on PC or your console dashboard. Beast of Reincarnation launches day one on Game Pass Ultimate, so there's no reason not to preload if you're subscribed.

A few things worth flagging:

- **16 GB RAM is the floor.** If you're still on 8 GB, you'll need an upgrade.

## 10 Things I Wish I Knew Before Starting

### 1. Learn to Parry Before Anything Else

Beast of Reincarnation runs on a Sekiro-like deflection system. The parry window sits at roughly **8 frames at 60 FPS** — tight, but learnable. Blocking isn't a substitute; regular blocks eat 30% chip damage and drain your stamina fast. Perfect parries give you zero damage, massive stagger buildup on the enemy, and a Blight Energy refund.

Pro tip from the Fextralife community: initiate a slight movement before raising your guard. Something about the animation blending makes parry timing feel noticeably more consistent than blocking from a static position.

### 2. Koo Is Your Co-Protagonist, Not a Pet

This isn't a game where your dog companion auto-attacks while you do the real work. You command Koo's **Bloom Arts** through a real-time tactical menu that slows combat without pausing it. His abilities include:

- **Distracting Bark** — draws aggro for 5 seconds (15s cooldown)
- **Hamstring Bite** — 40% enemy slow (20s cooldown)
- **Sealer's Howl** — 15% damage buff for Emma (45s cooldown)
- **Flora Sniff** — highlights hidden healing plants (60s cooldown)

Players who ignore Koo are fighting at half strength. The combat loop is designed around alternating between Emma's parries and Koo's commands. Use the command menu as a tactical breather — those few frames of slowdown are intentional, not a crutch.

### 3. Pick Your Difficulty Honestly

Three modes are available from the start. Director Kota Furushima has said the game is "built to be approachable," but the combat is still timing-based and punishing if you treat it like a hack-and-slash.

- **Story Mode** — ideal if you're here for the narrative and the world, or if action games aren't your usual genre
- **Standard** — the intended experience, challenging but fair
- **Hard** — tighter parry windows, more aggressive enemy AI, resource scarcity

You can switch mid-campaign, so there's no shame in starting on Standard and dropping down if a Nushi boss walls you.

### 4. Camps Are Checkpoints, Not Safe Havens

The checkpoint system works like bonfires in Dark Souls. Resting at a camp restores your health and refills resources — but it also respawns every enemy in the zone. Always bank your resources (spirit stones, Amber) before you rest, and don't use a camp just because you're at half health. Sometimes pushing forward to the next camp is the better call.

### 5. Explore the Blight Forests

Emma's plant-vine powers aren't just for combat — they're your traversal toolkit. You can double-jump, grapple to zip-lines, and create vine bridges across gaps. The blight forests that spawn around Nushi territories are the game's most hostile zones, but they're also where the best loot lives.

The prettiest, most dangerous-looking places tend to hide spirit stones, rare crafting materials, and optional bosses that drop unique charms for Koo. If a vine path looks possible, it almost always leads somewhere.

### 6. Don't Over-Commit Your Build Early

You shape Emma through three systems: katanas (moveset and weight), spirit stones (trigger on successful parries), and skill trees (branching paths toward ranged, stealthy, or aggressive playstyles). Koo uses equippable charms that modify his Bloom Arts.

In the first 5-10 hours, **experiment.** The early Nushi bosses give you a taste of different combat scenarios — some favor ranged chip damage, others demand aggressive parry-heavy play. Locking into a build before you've seen the variety is the fastest way to hit a wall.

### 7. Seal Everything You Kill

Every malefact you defeat is "sealed," feeding Emma's growth. This is your primary progression loop — not XP grinding, not currency farming. Bosses in particular grant captured powers that unlock entirely new abilities. Skipping fights isn't playing smart; it's leaving progression on the table.

### 8. FP Economy Beats Raw Damage

Focus Points (FP) are what let you issue Koo's commands. Many new players obsess over increasing weapon damage — but having constant FP means more staggers, more buffs, and more crowd control. Prioritize gear and skills that improve FP generation or reduce ability cooldowns. A 15% damage buff that you can activate twice as often is worth more than a 25% buff you can barely afford.

### 9. Save Buff Windows for Stagger States

Temporary buffs like Sealer's Howl last about 10-12 seconds. If you pop it the moment it's off cooldown, you'll waste half the duration repositioning or dodging. Instead, wait until you've staggered the enemy or created a clear attack window — then buff and go all-in. This is especially true against Nushi bosses where stagger windows are your only real damage opportunity.

### 10. Study Boss Phases Before Committing

The number one mistake I see: charging into a Nushi fight and dumping all your resources in the first 30 seconds. Every major boss has distinct phases, and phase 3 is almost always the real fight. Use your first attempt to:

- Learn the attack patterns and which moves are parryable
- Identify the one or two easily punishable attacks
- Conserve strong Bloom Arts for the later phases
- Avoid the classic "one hit left, let me just end this" panic that gets you killed

The Rangafer boss in particular has humbled a lot of early players who treated it like a DPS race. It's a patience check.

## FAQ

**When does Beast of Reincarnation release?**
August 4, 2026 at 00:00 UTC globally. This translates to the evening of August 3 for players in the Americas. The Steam store page shows August 3 for some regions due to timezone differences — both dates are correct and refer to the same unlock moment.

**Is Beast of Reincarnation on Game Pass?**
Yes, day one on Xbox Game Pass Ultimate (Console, PC, and Cloud). It also supports Xbox Play Anywhere, so buying on the Microsoft Store gets you both the Xbox and PC versions.

**Is Beast of Reincarnation a soulslike?**
It shares DNA — demanding parry-based combat, bonfire-style checkpoints, punishing bosses — but Game Freak calls it a "one-person, one-dog action RPG." The hybrid combat (real-time for Emma, command-based for Koo) and the three difficulty modes make it more approachable than a pure soulslike.

**How long is the campaign?**
Director Kota Furushima told Game Informer the main story spans roughly 10 major stages and about 30 hours. Completionist runs with all Nushi hunts and optional content will push well past 40.

**Can I play Beast of Reincarnation on PS5 Pro?**
Yes, the Japanese product listing confirms PlayStation 5 Pro Enhanced support with a dedicated presentation mode.

Dune: Awakening used to be one thing: a 75-hour open-world survival MMO where your worst enemy was another player with a stolen ornithopter. With Update 1.5 and the September 22 console launch, it is now two things, and the menu asks you to pick which one before you spawn. My first two evenings were messy because I kept playing it like Conan Exiles. Sprint across open sand, get swallowed by a worm, respawn, do it again. The mistake was not skill — it was treating Arrakis like a generic desert map. Once I stopped running on sand, chose the right class for solo play, and built a base inside a rock formation instead of on the open dunes, the game transformed. Below is everything I wish I had known before the character creation screen, organized so you can read it in order or jump to the system that is confusing you right now.

## Solo Mode vs. Multiplayer: Decide in the First Two Minutes

Update 1.5 turns Dune: Awakening into two parallel games. You pick one at the main menu, and the choice is effectively permanent: a Solo character cannot be promoted into a Multiplayer server, and Multiplayer characters cannot be moved into a Solo world. (You can move Multiplayer characters between Official, Private, and Self-Hosted servers, but not into Solo.)

The two modes share almost everything — every region, every mission, every weapon, every skill tree — but they trade different things.

## The Four Starting Classes — and Which One to Pick First

You can choose between four schools of the Imperium at character creation. The fifth — Planetologist — unlocks about an hour into the main story. None of these choices lock you out of the others permanently. Every school has trainer NPCs scattered across Hagga Basin and Jabal Eifrit; complete their quests and you unlock that school's skill tree, including active abilities and passives. There is also a respec system at level 20+ that lets you reset your allocation every 48 hours.

What your starting class really controls is your first ten hours: the active ability you have available when you die to your first sandworm.

### My Recommendation

If you are playing Solo Mode on console, pick <strong>Trooper</strong>. The Shigawire Claw gives you a panic button when you are surrounded, Trooper trainers unlock early at Griffin's Reach Outpost, and the ranged kit handles the most common early-game threat — distant bandits — without needing you to master parry timing.

Pick <strong>Bene Gesserit</strong> second. Once you have the Bindu Sprint passive, you can cross open sand in short bursts and survive the worm encounters that kill most new players. The Compel ability is also excellent in Solo because it pulls enemies off-balance before you commit to a melee combo.

Avoid starting with <strong>Swordmaster</strong> if you are brand-new to Soulslike combat. The Deflection parry is powerful, but the timing window is narrower than it looks, and a missed parry in Solo Mode still drops your durability by 2% on death.

The 200-skill-point cap means you can fully spec two schools and dabble in a third. A common Solo build is Trooper for active abilities + Planetologist for passive resource and survival bonuses.

## The First 60 Minutes: Water Is Your Real Health Bar

Arrakis is a survival game disguised as an MMO. The first thing the game teaches you is that your health bar is a lie. What actually matters is hydration.

In the opening hours, you will get hydration from <strong>Dew Flowers</strong> scattered across rocky outcrops. They refill roughly one-third of your bar, so plan to drink before you cross open sand. Overdrinking Dew Flowers causes nausea, which is a deliberate design choice — you are meant to transition to better sources quickly.

Your first major crafting priority is a <strong>Stillsuit</strong>. The Mk1 Stillsuit recycles body moisture into your hydration bar passively and is the single largest quality-of-life upgrade in the game. Without a Stillsuit, you will drink Dew Flowers until you are sick. With one, you can cross multiple regions before needing to refill.

### Heat and Sunstroke

Direct sunlight drains hydration faster and stacks a Sunstroke debuff that further reduces hydration and movement speed. The simplest counter is to <strong>travel at night</strong>. Arrakis nights are cold but not lethal, and the lower temperature means your Stillsuit cycles far more efficiently.

If you must travel by day, stick to rock formations, cave mouths, and canyon floors. The base-building section below covers how to use rock as a natural base wall — the same principle applies to movement.

### Sandworm Vibration Rules

Sandworms detect vibration. Running on open sand, jumping, vehicle engines, and even combat all spike your vibration meter. When it turns red, the worm is already en route. You have about four seconds to reach solid rock.

<ProTip title="Walk on sand; run on rock">The single biggest mistake new players make is sprinting across an open dune thinking they are saving time. You are saving zero seconds — the worm reaches you before you reach the next rock formation. Walk everywhere on sand. Run only on stone.</ProTip>

Carry a <strong>Thumper</strong> in your inventory from around hour three onward. A Thumper emits rhythmic vibration that lures worms away from your escape path. It is a panic button, not a routine tool, and you should never use one unless you are already being chased or are actively looting something high-value on open sand.

For Solo Mode, a <strong>rock overhang</strong> is the safest starter base. You get a natural ceiling that blocks Coriolis storm damage, natural walls that worms cannot penetrate, and shade that keeps your hydration loss low when you walk outside. Update 1.5 lets you rotate and elevate the Sub-Fief Console to fit uneven terrain, so do not give up on a rocky spot just because the ground is bumpy.

### Essential Structures (in This Order)

1. <strong>Sub-Fief Console</strong> — claims your land and enables building rights.
2. <strong>Power Generator</strong> — keeps the shield up against storms. Without power, your base degrades.
3. <strong>Fabricator</strong> — the main crafting station for armor, tools, and vehicles.
4. <strong>Blood Purifier</strong> — converts enemy blood into drinkable water. Pair with a Stillsuit and you have a self-sustaining hydration loop.
5. <strong>Ore Refinery</strong> — processes raw materials into usable ingots.
6. <strong>Storage Units</strong> — protect valuable resources and crafted items from raid losses.
7. <strong>Vehicle Bay</strong> — store a Sandbike or Ornithopter inside, or it will be destroyed by the next storm.
8. <strong>Backup Generator</strong> — fuel cells deplete fast. A secondary generator prevents power loss during critical moments.

Update 1.5 also adds a <strong>Base Reconstruction Tool</strong> that stores up to three complete base snapshots. If your base is destroyed, you can rebuild it instantly from the snapshot instead of laying every wall down manually. Use this.

## Movement, Vehicles, and the PvP Decision

### Sandbike First, Ornithopter Later

Your first vehicle should be a Sandbike. It is cheap, fast on rock, and quiet enough on open sand to avoid worm detection if you drive at low throttle. The Ornithopter is the iconic Dune aircraft but it is loud, fragile, and expensive to maintain — unlock it around hour ten, not hour one.

Park vehicles inside your base or under a rock overhang, never on open sand. The next Coriolis storm will destroy anything left outside. Update 1.5 lets you recover destroyed vehicles within 30 days at a Solari cost and a durability penalty; it is not free, but it is far cheaper than rebuilding.

### PvE-Only Hagga Basin vs. Contested Deep Desert

On Solo Mode and on Official PvE servers, Hagga Basin is fully PvE. Shipwrecks in Hagga no longer block critical crafting resources after PvP fights, which means the entire early-game progression loop can be completed without firing a shot at another player.

The Deep Desert is split into PvE and PvP instances on Official servers. PvP pays 2.5× the normal yield from mining and harvesting — a meaningful endgame grind boost — but you will lose vehicles and base progress to organized guilds. In Solo Mode, you can access the same Deep Desert content without the risk.

The Landsraad endgame now uses <strong>repeatable missions</strong> covering combat, crafting, gathering, exploration, and sabotage. You progress through Landsraad ranks without fighting another player, then enter the contested PvP Deep Desert only when you actively want the 2.5× yield bonus.

<ProTip title="Returning player gift">Anyone who logs in between September 17 and October 22 receives the Matron's Decor reward. Anyone returning after 28+ days gets a research-tier welcome-back package that scales to your previous progress.</ProTip>

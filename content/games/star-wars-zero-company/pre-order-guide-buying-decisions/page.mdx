## What's in the Pre-Order Bonus

Every pre-order, Standard or Deluxe, unlocks the **Crystalline Astromech Cosmetic Pack**. The pack includes:

- A full **R3 droid body** (a new astromech design)
- Translucent **crystalline heads** for the R4 and R5 astromech variants
- The **BR-1 droid**, making its first ever appearance in a Star Wars game

It is cosmetic only — no mechanical gameplay advantage — but the BR-1 is a fun collector's item for Star Wars diehards.

## The Story: Twilight of the Clone Wars, From the Shadows

You play as **Hawks**, a disgraced Republic officer whose appearance, gender, and species you customize at the start. The year is **20 BBY** — one year before the events of *Revenge of the Sith* — and the official Clone Wars narrative is reaching its violent end. Zero Company is something else entirely: a mercenary outfit drawn from across the galaxy, working with Republic Intelligence on operations that will never make the history books.

The story's primary antagonist is the **Infinite Coil**, a Separatist-linked paramilitary cult spreading a mysterious disease called the **Shadow Plague**. Infected survivors transform into unpredictable, dangerous soldiers — the tactical layer of every mission.

## The Squad: Five Named Characters, Plus Recruits

Zero Company is not a nameless roster. You command five fully written story characters, each with unique abilities:

- **Trick** — Clone trooper with heavy weapons access. Unlocks a rocket launcher ultimate after building 10 Advantage.
- **Tel-Rea Vokoss** — Jedi Padawan. Can use the Force to push enemies into environmental hazards.
- **Kabb** — One-armed brawler. Closes distance fast and knocks enemies into allies' lines of fire.
- **Luco Bronc** — Umbaran sniper. Long-range elimination and overwatch specialist.
- **Jae Mordant** — Exiled aristocrat. Versatile support role.
- **Cly Kullervo** — Mandalorian gunslinger. Dual-blaster DPS with crowd control.

Between missions, you also recruit from a **procedurally generated pool** of mercenaries across a range of classes. Story characters can die to injuries (toggleable), but losing one ends their personal questline permanently — making every deployment a real risk.

## How Combat Actually Feels

If you have played **XCOM 2** or **Marvel's Midnight Suns**, you already know the shape. Each operative gets **3 Action Points** per turn, spent on movement, attacks, abilities, or overwatch. Cover, flanking, and percentage-based hit rolls are all present.

What is new in Zero Company:

- **Backup system** — Operatives can call an ally for a free shot if they have line of sight. Chained correctly, this creates multi-hit combos in a single turn.
- **Advantage meter** — A shared resource your squad builds by damaging enemies. Spend it on game-changing ultimates (Trick's rocket, extra AP, accuracy buffs, taunts).
- **Bonds** — Healing, using abilities on, or fighting alongside a squadmate grows their Bond, unlocking permanent Cross Training perks.
- **No fog of war** — Enemies are visible from the start of combat, making positioning predictable. Tradeoff: less ambush tension, more tactical planning.

PC Gamer called Zero Company "the closest big-budget XCOM successor strategy fans have had in years." Pure Xbox and RPG Site highlighted how authored characters plus optional permadeath make squadmate losses feel meaningful in a way that XCOM's anonymous soldiers could not.

<ProTip title="Disable Permadeath on a First Run">
The "ironman" experience is where Zero Company shines, but if this is your first tactics game in years, start with permadeath off. Story characters dying early will lock you out of quest content, and you cannot easily replay to recover it.
</ProTip>

## Who Should Buy on Day One

**Buy if you are:**
- An XCOM 2 or Midnight Suns fan who has been waiting for a true successor
- A Star Wars fan interested in the **shadow war** beneath the Clone Wars (the "Rogue One" angle)
- A tactics newcomer who wants a more forgiving, story-driven entry point than XCOM

**Wait for a sale if you are:**
- Strictly a Star Wars fan uninterested in turn-based combat
- Already on the fence — this is a single-player, no-multiplayer release that will hold its value through sales
- Looking for a quick 10-hour experience. Zero Company is **25+ hours** for the main campaign, longer with optional objectives

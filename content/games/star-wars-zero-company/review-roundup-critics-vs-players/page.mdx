*Star Wars Zero Company* launched **August 27, 2026**, and immediately pulled off something no Star Wars tactics game has ever managed: it became one of the highest-rated games of the year. Metacritic has it at **86** from 43 critic reviews, OpenCritic's top critics average sits at **88** with a **91% recommendation rate**, and 42 of 43 scored reviews are positive — not a single outright pan in the batch.

Then you open Steam, where the user rating sits around **75% positive** — "Mostly Positive," with roughly one in four players declining to recommend the game they are, by many accounts, actively enjoying.

Both things are true at once, and the gap between them is the most important thing to understand before you buy. This roundup covers what critics scored, why players are cooler, and a clear verdict by platform.

The praise is strikingly consistent. IGN called it "an excellent tactical game" with the cinematic feel of Respawn's *Jedi* series and a refreshingly non-Force-user story. PC Gamer declared it "a new all-timer Star Wars game and a stunning debut for Bit Reactor." The recurring through-line: tactical combat worthy of the Firaxis pedigree (Bit Reactor was founded by former XCOM developers), a fixed cast of characters with genuine *Mass Effect*-style chemistry, and a Clone Wars setting that treats the license as a story about people rather than a merchandise catalog.

The critic complaints are real but measured: a linear, handcrafted-only campaign of roughly 30-40 hours with no procedural maps, difficulty that skilled XCOM players will outgrow on normal, no official mod support, and technical rough edges.

## What Steam Players Are Saying

The user score opened at roughly **69% positive** within hours of launch and has since climbed to around 75-78% across 3,000+ reviews — a recovering, but clearly split, reception. Meanwhile, the game peaked near **69,000 concurrent players on Steam**, beating *Star Wars Jedi: Survivor*'s all-time high for the franchise on the platform. People are buying it and playing it in force.

The negative reviews are not about the game underneath. They are about the layer on top of it:

**Performance and crashes.** This is an Unreal Engine 5 game with demanding requirements — the official recommended spec for 1440p/60 on High is an RTX 3080 or Radeon RX 7800 XT with 32GB of RAM, startling numbers for a turn-based tactics title. Independent testing across five different machines found a different set of crashes, freezes, and lockups on each one — including on an RTX 5090 desktop. PC Gamer's reviewer hit two hard crashes and a severe graphical glitch requiring a save reload. The day-zero patch helped some players and changed little for others.

**Bugs and jank.** Camera issues during attack zoom-ins, missing animations, dead enemies standing upright until end of turn, flickering UI, long hangs while enemy turns resolve.

**Steam Deck owners are out of luck.** Bottomed-out settings plus FSR yields a 15-20fps slideshow. This is not a handheld game in its current state.

<ProTip title="The community's +40 FPS fix">The most-upvoted Steam review circulating since launch recommends fully disabling the "Environment Geometry Detail" option, which alone recovered roughly 40 FPS for its author. If you are struggling on PC, try that setting first — results vary by machine, but the gains reported are significant.</ProTip>

## Why the Divide Exists

Critics reviewed a game. Players reviewed a launch. Review copies were tested on controlled hardware, several reviewers noted post-patch improvement, and review builds did not carry every configuration's quirks. The public bought the same game across an infinite sprawl of PC configurations and hit UE5 instability that reviews sampled but did not fully surface.

The tell is in what players praise when they recommend it: the tactical combat, the squad chemistry, the dual-specialization builds, the *Rogue One*-toned story. Even the harshest negative reviews concede the game underneath is good. This is the inverse of most review-bombing stories — the audience isn't angry at what the game is, only at how it runs.

## Should You Buy Now?

**On PS5 or Xbox Series X|S — yes, buy with confidence.** The critic consensus reflects the console experience, the technical complaints skew heavily toward PC, and the $59.99 console Standard edition is fairly priced for a 30-40 hour single-player campaign with no live-service strings. Our full edition breakdown is in the [pre-order guide](/games/star-wars-zero-company/pre-order-guide-buying-decisions).

**On a high-end PC — likely fine, buy when ready.** Plenty of RTX 40/50-class rigs are running it well above 60 FPS. Apply the settings tip above, and see our [crash and permadeath warning guide](/games/star-wars-zero-company/launch-day-crash-permadeath-warning) before your first session.

**On a mid-range or older PC — wait for patches.** If you are below the recommended spec, or you hoped to play on Steam Deck or a handheld, the current build will fight you. Bit Reactor shipped a day-zero patch and the trend line is improving — the user score has already climbed nearly ten points since launch morning. A week or two of patches is a reasonable wait.

**New to the genre?** The game is friendlier than XCOM — story difficulty exists, permadeath is optional, and our [beginner tactics guide](/games/star-wars-zero-company/beginner-tactics-guide-tips) covers the systems that matter in your first hours.

<InfoBox title="Bottom line">86 on Metacritic is not a mirage — it is one of the best-reviewed games of 2026, and the strongest Star Wars game reception in years. The Steam grumbling is a performance tax, not a quality verdict. Console players should dive in now; PC players below recommended spec should give Bit Reactor a couple of weeks to smooth out the UE5 edges.</InfoBox>

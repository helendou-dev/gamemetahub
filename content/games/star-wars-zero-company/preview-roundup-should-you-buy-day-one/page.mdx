### What Critics Agree On

**The pros:** cinematic presentation worthy of the Star Wars films, genuinely distinct squad members, and a grounded Clone Wars story that earns its Andor comparisons. Multiple reviewers called it the best-funded tactics game in years — a genre that rarely gets this level of production value.

**The cons:** the mission-variety concern from the preview wave carried into final reviews — objectives still lean heavily on "eliminate all" and "evacuate" formats. And Shacknews' 70 represents the minority verdict that the story pacing sags between missions.

### ⚠️ The Launch Bug Every Permadeath Player Must Know

Reviewers flagged one critical technical issue: **crash bugs that can wipe permadeath campaign progress**. If you are playing with permadeath on — the way the game is arguably meant to be played — save manually and often until the day-one patch settles. Also note: the Steam Deck version reportedly runs at 15-20 fps and is effectively unplayable at launch, and review copies did not include the day-one patch, so launch-week stability may differ from the review builds.

## The One Weakness Every Buyer Should Know

Game Informer raised it most explicitly: **mission objective variety**. Objectives frequently default to "eliminate all enemies" or "reach a point of interest and evacuate while reinforcements spawn" — proven formats, but repetitive when stacked back to back.

The counterpoint: structurally distinctive missions do exist. An espionage sequence where Hawks dons an officer's uniform to infiltrate a restricted facility was cited as a highlight. Final reviews confirmed the concern was real but not game-breaking — it is the most common deduction in the 80-85 score band, not a dealbreaker.

## Why the XCOM Comparison Is Accurate but Incomplete

Zero Company inherits the genre skeleton — squad of four to six, turn-based tactical maps, permanent death when a character falls — but makes three architectural changes:

- **Shared 3-AP pool** — Unlike XCOM's separate move/action budgets, every character draws movement, attacks, and abilities from one three-point pool per turn. The result is more flexible combo planning.
- **Cone-based Overwatch** — Overwatch only covers an adjustable cone, and hit chance scales with how wide you set it. You trade coverage for accuracy, making reaction fire a tactical decision rather than a free default.
- **No fog of war** — Enemies are visible from combat start. You lose ambush tension but gain full-information tactical planning, which suits the game's grounded war-story tone.

That tone is the other differentiator. The references the developers cite are *The Dirty Dozen*, *Andor*, and *Rogue One* — wartime desperation and moral ambiguity, not Jedi heroics. You play **Hawks**, a disgraced Republic officer running mercenary contracts during the twilight of the Clone Wars, hunted by the **Infinite Coil**, a cult whose Shadow Plague grants strength at the cost of free will — and whose fallen soldiers transfer their powers to nearby allies, multiplying under pressure instead of diminishing.

## Score Prediction vs. Reality: The Mid-80s Call Was Right

Before the embargo, ResetEra's prediction thread skewed heavily toward the **mid-to-high 80s**, in line with the pedigree — Bit Reactor's founders shipped XCOM: Enemy Unknown (Metacritic 89), XCOM 2 (88), Civilization VI, and Marvel's Midnight Suns at Firaxis.

The final answer: **Metacritic 86, OpenCritic 87, 89% recommended.** The prediction landed almost exactly. The flagged risk factors — mission-variety fatigue and day-one bugs — both materialized in reviews as deductions, not disqualifiers: the former caps most scores in the 80-85 band, and the latter is what the day-one patch is for.

**Star Wars Zero Company** is live today, August 27, on PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S — and the first review wave has landed on a surprising consensus: Bit Reactor's XCOM-style Clone Wars tactics game is being called one of the best Star Wars titles in years, while simultaneously carrying a launch-day technical risk that permadeath players need to understand before committing. [PC Gamer handed it a 90/100](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/star-wars-zero-company-review) and called it "a new all-timer Star Wars game"; Game Informer scored it 8/10. But the same reviews document crashes, an unfriendly Steam Deck experience, and a day-one patch that critics never got to test.

Here's the full picture before you hit buy.

## The Crash Problem Is Different With Permadeath

The most serious issue comes from Empire's review, which flagged mid-battle crashes that erased progress entirely — a categorically different problem for Zero Company than for most games. When a character dies in this game, that loss is the core emotional mechanic. When a crash wipes an in-progress battle, you lose that consequence without the drama, along with any mission progress and potentially the squad you were fighting to protect.

PC Gamer independently confirmed two hard crashes and one severe graphical glitch that forced a save reload during its playthrough. Gamereactor also noted persistent texture pop-in and missions that occasionally required restarts. None of these stopped reviewers from praising the game — but for a title built around permanent loss, a crash is a uniquely expensive failure mode.

## Not a Steam Deck Candidate

If you were planning to play on Valve's handheld, temper expectations. PC Gamer's testing bottomed out every setting and maxed FSR, resulting in "a soupy, unparsable, 15-20fps mess." Bit Reactor has given no indication the Deck is a supported target, and at those framerates a turn-based tactics game is playable but unpleasant.

## The Day-One Patch Question

EA has confirmed a day-one patch ships with launch, but critics did not have access to it during the review period. That means the crashes, pop-in, and camera issues documented in reviews may already be addressed in the build you download today — or may not be. The safe play: check the post-launch patch notes or the community hub before a permadeath run, and consider backing up saves early if you're on PC.

## The Good News

Everything else is being praised: the dual-class customization, the Astromech support units, a story that reviewers are comparing to Mass Effect and Rogue One, and roughly 30-40 hours of handcrafted, linear campaign content. There's no mod support planned and replayability is thinner than XCOM 2's sandbox, but as a single campaign it's landing as a legitimate Game of the Year contender. If you already locked in a copy, the core game underneath the technical noise is very much real. Missed the pre-order window? [Our breakdown of the editions and what's actually worth paying for](/games/star-wars-zero-company/pre-order-guide-buying-decisions) covers the value question.

## What to Watch

Keep an eye on patch 1.01's notes in the next few days. If EA's day-one patch resolves the crash reports, the remaining complaints are minor. If permadeath-wiping crashes persist, waiting a week costs you nothing — and the game isn't going anywhere.

## I Restarted BG3 Four Times Before It Clicked

My first character was a Wizard. I picked bad spells. I had 10 Constitution. I died to intellect devourers on the beach. Three times. I restarted as a Rogue, realized I didn't understand Sneak Attack, and died to the same intellect devourers. Restarted again as a Druid. Wild Shaped into a badger. Got one-shot.

Fourth character: Paladin. Heavy armor, Divine Smite, Charisma for dialogue. Suddenly the game made sense. I wasn't dying constantly and NPCs actually liked me.

200+ hours later across multiple playthroughs, here's what I wish someone had told me during those first frustrating hours.

**Three things that would have saved me 20 hours:**
- **Pick a Charisma class for your main character.** Paladin, Bard, Sorcerer, or Warlock. You'll pass more dialogue checks, unlock more story paths, and get better merchant prices. Charisma is the "protagonist stat."
- **You can respec everything.** Withers shows up about 2 hours in and lets you change your class, stats, and spells for 100 gold. Nothing about your build is permanent. Stop stressing about optimization.
- **Long Rest constantly.** The game screams "YOU HAVE A TADPOLE IN YOUR BRAIN, HURRY" but almost nothing is actually timed. Long resting triggers companion scenes and romance progression. If you push through 10 fights without resting, you miss half the character content.

## D&D Basics: 30 Seconds, I Promise

BG3 runs on Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition rules. If you've never touched D&D:

- **6 ability scores:** Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. Each class has 1-2 primary scores. Your class page highlights them.
- **Everything is a d20:** Attack rolls, skill checks, saving throws. Roll a 20-sided die, add your ability modifier, compare to the target number. Higher = better.
- **Advantage is huge:** Rolling with advantage means roll twice, take the higher. Roughly equals a +5 bonus. Getting advantage on attacks is more important than stacking small stat bonuses.

That's literally all you need to know. The rest you'll pick up naturally.

### What You Can Change vs What's Permanent

| Permanent | Respec'able |
|-----------|-------------|
| Race (without mods) | Class & Subclass |
| Background | Ability Scores |
| Body type | Feats & Spells |
| Origin character choice | Everything else |

Your race and background are the only choices you can't undo. Everything else costs 100 gold at camp.

## Best Classes for Beginners, Honestly Ranked

### S-Tier: Pick These and Have Fun

**1. Paladin — The "I Want to Win" Class**

Heavy armor, high AC, Divine Smite deletes enemies, Lay on Hands heals yourself. Oath of Vengeance is the most straightforward subclass — no complicated oath mechanics, just point at bad guys and kill them.

Paladins are Charisma-based so you'll pass dialogue checks. They're tanky so you won't die constantly. Divine Smite triggers on Critical Hits for double damage dice, and watching a goblin boss explode from a 50-damage single hit never gets old.

One trap to avoid: don't waste spell slots on actual spells. Use them for Divine Smite. Smite on crits for double dice. Smite is your entire personality.

**2. Fighter (Battle Master) — Press Button, Deal Damage**

The simplest combat class. Heavy armor, all weapons, extra attacks at levels 5 and 11, and Action Surge at level 2 lets you take two full actions in one turn. Battle Master maneuvers add tactical options without complexity.

Pick Precision Attack, Riposte, and Trip Attack as your first maneuvers. They work regardless of your weapon and always feel useful. The only downside: Fighters have low Charisma, so you'll want a companion for dialogue. You're the muscle, not the face.

### A-Tier: Slightly More to Learn

**3. Warlock (The Fiend) — Eldritch Blast Is a Lifestyle**

The best cantrip in the game. Warlocks buff Eldritch Blast to add Charisma damage and push enemies off cliffs. Fewer spell slots means less decision paralysis — cast one big spell, then spam Eldritch Blast.

At level 2, take Agonizing Blast and Repelling Blast as your Eldritch Invocations. These are non-negotiable. Without them, you're just a worse Wizard with fewer spells. The Fiend patron gives temporary HP on every kill, which is great survivability for a class that can't wear heavy armor.

**4. Ranger (Gloom Stalker) — The Fixed One**

Ranger had a bad reputation in tabletop D&D, but BG3's version is actually strong. Gloom Stalker gives an extra opening-round attack, bonus initiative, and darkvision. Dual hand crossbows with the Sharpshooter feat at level 4 outputs absurd damage.

At character creation, pick "Bounty Hunter" as Favored Enemy — the Ensnaring Strike bonus is fantastic early game. Skip "Beast Tamer" — the animal companion is useless after level 3.

### B-Tier: Good But Requires More Effort

**5. Cleric (Life Domain) — The Healer**

Life Cleric is the best healer, full stop. Heavy armor proficiency, massive healing output, and at level 5 you get Spirit Guardians — a radiant damage aura that melts undead while you heal.

The trap: playing pure support is boring. New players waste spell slots buffing the party before combat when they should be casting Spirit Guardians, taking the Dodge action, and walking into enemies. You don't need to attack. The radiant blender does all the work. It's genuinely funny to watch.

**6. Barbarian (Berserker) — Rage, Smash, Repeat**

Simple: Rage → attack → win. High HP, damage resistance while raging, and throwing enemies off cliffs is endlessly satisfying. Carry javelins or handaxes — thrown weapons use Strength and benefit from Rage damage, so you've got a ranged option without needing Dexterity.

The downside: Barbarians are boring in dialogue because their Charisma is terrible, and Rage management gets frustrating if you Long Rest infrequently. You have limited Rage charges per day and running out mid-dungeon is painful.

### Skip These on Your First Run

**Wizard, Druid, and Monk** are not bad classes — they just require system knowledge to play effectively:

- **Wizard:** Massive spell list, easy to pick terrible spells, dies if anything looks at them. You need to understand spell save DCs, concentration, and which of the 300 spells are actually good.
- **Druid:** Wild Shape rules are confusing, the spell list overlaps with Cleric but is less intuitive, and Moon Druid requires knowing which animal forms are worth using (hint: not badger).
- **Monk:** Ki point management, positioning-dependent damage, weakest early game of any martial class. Great at level 5+, miserable before that.

## Party Composition That Actually Works

You have 4 party slots including your character. This setup covers everything:

| Role | Companion | Why |
|------|-----------|-----|
| Face (dialogue) | **You** (Paladin/Warlock/Bard) | Charisma for story outcomes |
| Frontline damage | **Lae'zel** (Battle Master Fighter) | Best damage + survivability |
| Locks & traps | **Astarion** (Thief Rogue) | Expertise in Sleight of Hand, big Sneak Attack damage |
| Healer + support | **Shadowheart** (respec to Life Cleric at Withers) | Trickery Domain is weak — respec her immediately |

This gives you Charisma for dialogue, a frontline killer, a skill monkey for traps and chests, and a healer. Shadowheart comes with Guidance — a cantrip that adds 1d4 to every skill check. Bind it to a hotkey. You'll use it constantly.

## 10 Things the Tutorial Doesn't Mention (But Will Change Your Life)

### 1. Long Rest Way More Than You Think

The game screams about the tadpole. Ignore it. Almost nothing is timed. The only quest with an actual timer in Act 1 is a burning building — everything else waits for you. I Long Rest after every 2-3 fights and have never missed content. The opposite — I missed half the companion scenes on my first run because I wasn't resting enough.

### 2. Shove Kills Things Instantly

Shove costs a bonus action, not a full action. Enemies near ledges, cliffs, or lava die instantly to a single shove. Lae'zel with high Strength can push most humanoids. The goblin camp in Act 1 has at least 8 instant-kill shove spots. This isn't a gimmick — it's one of the strongest tools in the game and most new players ignore it.

### 3. Jump Triples Your Movement

Jumping costs a bonus action + 10 feet of movement, but a high-Strength character can jump 30+ feet — tripling their effective range. Use it to reposition behind enemies, reach high ground without climbing, or cross hazards. Karlach jumping across an entire battlefield to land directly behind a caster is both effective and hilarious.

### 4. Hold Alt to See Everything

Hold Left Alt to highlight every interactable object on screen. Containers, bodies, levers, traps, loose items. I walked past dozens of treasure chests in my first 20 hours because I didn't know this. Make it a habit: enter a room, hold Alt, scan, then move on.

### 5. Throw Healing Potions to Revive Allies

Healing potions can be thrown at downed allies to revive them, or at the ground between two injured characters to heal both. It's faster than drinking, doesn't require being adjacent, and Karlach with the Tavern Brawler feat becomes a shockingly effective field medic.

### 6. Talk to Every Animal and Every Corpse

Every animal can be spoken to. Every corpse can be questioned. These are not flavor mechanics — animals give quest hints, reveal hidden passages, and warn about ambushes. Dead NPCs reveal murderer identities and treasure locations. Speak With Animals is a ritual (free to cast outside combat). Speak With Dead is learned from a book in the Dank Crypt in Act 1. Get both.

### 7. Surprise Rounds Win Fights Before They Start

Attack while hidden and undetected to trigger a "surprise round" — enemies skip their entire first turn. Enter turn-based mode while stealthed, position each character separately, and open with a ranged attack. If no one is detected before the first hit lands, the entire enemy group is surprised.

### 8. Fix Your Action Bars

The default skill bar layout is terrible. Jump is in slot 3 while your subclass feature is buried on page 4. Drag everything around. Group similar abilities — all healing on one row, all attacks on another. Spend 5 minutes setting this up and you'll play 30% faster.

### 9. Respec Your Companions Immediately

Companion default stats are bad. Shadowheart has 13 Strength and 13 Dexterity — she's mediocre at everything. Respec her to 8 Str / 14 Dex / 16 Con / 8 Int / 16 Wis / 12 Cha as Life Cleric. Wyll's starting Dexterity is too low for light armor. Astarion needs Strength dumped for more Dexterity. Spend 500 gold respeccing your party and they'll perform twice as well.

### 10. Quicksave Is Your Friend — Use It

F5 quicksaves, F8 quickloads. Before every dialogue. Before every major fight. After every hard fight. Larian expects you to reload choices — some of the most interesting story outcomes require specific dice rolls and you don't get a do-over without saves. There's no penalty. Don't feel bad. Save often.

## Two Multiclass Builds That Won't Ruin Your Character

Multiclassing without a plan creates weak, directionless characters. These two combos are safe and powerful.

### The "Lockadin" — Paladin 7 / Warlock 5

The best melee build in the game. Pact of the Blade lets you use Charisma for weapon attacks instead of Strength, so you only need Charisma and Constitution. Warlock spell slots regenerate on Short Rest, giving you Divine Smite fuel for every fight. At level 12 you attack 3 times per turn (Extra Attack from both classes stacks in BG3), and every hit can Smite for up to 5d8 radiant damage.

**Leveling:** Paladin 1-5 (get Extra Attack first) → Warlock 1-5 (Fiend patron, Pact of the Blade at Warlock 3) → Paladin 6-7 (Aura of Protection).

### The "Gloomstalker Assassin" — Ranger 5 / Rogue 4 / Fighter 3

Opening-round nova damage. Dread Ambusher (Gloom Stalker) gives an extra attack. Assassin (Rogue) gives auto-crits against surprised enemies. Action Surge (Fighter 2) gives another full action. In the surprise round: 6 attacks, all at advantage, all critical hits, Sharpshooter + Hunter's Mark. First-round damage exceeds 300 by level 12.

**Leveling:** Ranger 1-5 (Gloom Stalker, Archery style, Sharpshooter at 4) → Rogue 1-4 (Assassin, +2 Dex at 4) → Fighter 1-3 (Battle Master, Precision Attack).

## Mistakes I Made So You Don't Have To

**I sold the Ring of Flinging for 40 gold.** It's a green-quality ring from the Druid Grove that adds 1d4 to thrown weapon damage. Pair it with the Returning Pike and Tavern Brawler on a Barbarian and it's a build that carries you through Act 2. Read item descriptions before selling anything.

**I ignored the environment for 30 hours.** Barrels of oil and smokepowder are everywhere. Chandeliers drop on enemies with one arrow. Grease bottles create terrain you can ignite. The environment is your strongest weapon and I treated it as decoration.

**I hoarded consumables like a dragon.** Finished my first playthrough with 47 unused scrolls and 23 potions of Speed. Speed potions, invisibility potions, scrolls of Fireball — use them. The game drowns you in consumables. Every fight you finish with unused items is a fight you made harder than necessary.

**I multiclassed too early.** Never multiclass before level 5. Extra Attack and 3rd-level spells (Fireball, Spirit Guardians, Haste) are the biggest power spikes in the game. Delaying them by even one level makes Act 1's final boss dramatically harder.

**I didn't use high ground.** Ranged attacks from elevation get +2 to hit, enemies below get -2. That's a 20% accuracy swing. The goblin camp fight is nearly impossible on the ground and target practice from the rafters. Always start combat by moving archers and casters to high ground.

## FAQ

### Which difficulty?

**Balanced.** Explorer mode prevents multiclassing and reduces enemy HP by 30% — you'll miss build variety. Tactician is genuinely punishing without system knowledge. Balanced hits the sweet spot: you can make mistakes without dying, but combat requires thinking.

### Do I need to play BG1 and BG2?

No. BG3 takes place 120 years later with a mostly separate story. There are returning characters (Jaheira, Minsc) but the game explains everything. I never played the originals and followed the plot fine.

### How long is it?

Main story: 60-80 hours with reasonable side content. Completionist: 120-150 hours. Speedrun: 30-40 hours. The game rewards exploration — most of the best items and companion moments are in optional areas.

### Is every line voiced?

Yes. Every dialogue line, including narration and internal monologue, is fully voiced. Named NPCs alone have 1.5 million words of recorded dialogue. The narrator (Amelia Tyler) is phenomenal.

### Best companions for story?

Shadowheart and Lae'zel have the most main-plot integration. Astarion has the best personal quest arc. Gale peaks in Act 2. Wyll and Karlach have great moments but less plot relevance. If you can only commit to three, take Shadowheart, Lae'zel, and Astarion for maximum story density.

### Does race matter?

More than most games. Drow get unique goblin interactions (they're terrified of you). Githyanki get massive Lae'zel story content. Tieflings face prejudice. Dragonborn get almost nothing — sorry, dragon people. For the most unique dialogue, play Drow or Githyanki.

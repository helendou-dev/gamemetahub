Team NINJA's first expansion for Nioh 3, **Hell Rising**, drops tomorrow — **Tuesday, August 18 at 9:00 PM PT** on PS5 and PC (Steam). Six months after the base game's February launch, this is the moment Nioh 3 gets meaningfully harder, and a lot more interesting.

I've been running through what's confirmed so far so you don't have to dig through a dozen press releases. Here's everything in Hell Rising — the new setting, the new weapon class, the new difficulty ceiling, and the prep checklist that actually matters before you log in.

## What Hell Rising Is: Keian-Era Edo, 1651

Hell Rising picks up with protagonist Takechiyo hurled forward in time to **Edo, 1651 — the opening years of the Keian era**. The city should be Japan at its settled peak; instead, the death of the future shogun has plunged the castle town into chaos, and the Crucible's cursed gales are tearing through it once more.

The new open-field map is described as a hellish wasteland where yokai, human rebels, and foreign alchemists all roam. Two characters anchor the new story arc:

- **Yagyu Jubei** — the legendary one-eyed swordsman, and a natural fit for Nioh 3's historical-fiction DNA.
- **Rodrigo** — a Spanish envoy with his own stake in the Spirit Stone mysteries behind the crisis.

That historical-figure-meets-foreign-arrival pairing is classic Nioh — history colliding with the outside world — and Koei Tecmo is leaning into it hard here.

<InfoBox title="Series Context">
The base game's story ran through 1622 Edo with Takechiyo and Kunimatsu. Hell Rising jumps roughly three decades forward — so if you've finished the main campaign, you're ready for the time skip. The DLC assumes you've completed the base game's story.
</InfoBox>

## The Hoko & Shield: A Brand-New Weapon Class

The headline feature is a **new weapon archetype: the Hoko & Shield**, available in both the Samurai and Ninja styles. The design blends the short spear's reach and thrusting power with a shield's blocking capability — a defensive, guard-counter playstyle that Nioh 3's existing arsenal doesn't quite cover.

Two details stand out from the official breakdown:

1. **Basic attacks combine shield defense with spear offense** — you can hold the line and poke from behind cover, which changes how you approach human enemies that previously punished defensive play.
2. **Tactical moves include flinging the shield at enemies** — a ranged option that opens up openings for follow-ups, and honestly the most intriguing part for build theorycrafting.

For veterans, this is the first real question mark in the new meta: does Hoko & Shield slot into a tanky Samurai guard-counter build, or does Ninja-style mobility let you do hit-and-run shield tosses? We'll be testing both once it unlocks — expect our build breakdown shortly after launch.

Beyond the weapon, Hell Rising adds new **Martial Arts, Ninjutsu, Guardian Spirits, and Soul Cores**, giving every existing build fresh options on top of what carries over from the base game.

## New Endgame: Enlightened One's Journey and the Picture Scroll

Team NINJA is pushing the ceiling in two directions at once.

**Enlightened One's Journey** is a new difficulty slot above the base game's top tier, aimed at a third playthrough. Enemies can randomly receive a **Curse buff** that makes them significantly stronger — the kind of mode designed for players who have already burned through everything. The reward is real though: cursed enemies drop **divine-rarity items**, and the DLC adds divine-rarity Grace set bonuses for endgame build customization.

**The Hundred Demon Realms Picture Scroll** is a brand-new equipment category. Enemies sometimes drop a scroll as rare loot, but to equip it you must first clear the automatically generated battle bound to that scroll. Scrolls are replayable — each run lets you randomly re-roll one of the scroll's special effects while strengthening it. If you loved Nioh 2's scroll farming, this is that loop, formalized and expanded.

## Pre-Launch Checklist: What to Do Before Tomorrow

Here's what I'd actually do in the hours before the unlock — nothing here requires farming for 10 hours, it's all about being in the right state to jump in:

1. **Finish the main campaign (if you haven't).** The DLC assumes base-game completion. If you skipped side content, at least get the story done so you can access the new arc immediately.

2. **Check your save's difficulty progression.** Enlightened One's Journey targets a third playthrough, so make sure you've started your second cycle. Per series tradition, new difficulties unlock after clearing the previous tier.

3. **Bank materials, not just Amrita.** New weapons mean new upgrades. Hold onto smithing materials and soul cores instead of dumping them into your current weapon's final affixes.

4. **Pre-download on your platform.** The DLC is a digital unlock — make sure the base game is updated and pre-load is done so you're not waiting on a download when it drops at 9:00 PM PT.

5. **Decide your Hoko & Shield build early.** Samurai style leans into guard-counter and tanking; Ninja style leans into mobility and shield-toss setups. Picking one on day one saves you respec time.

## Price and How to Get It

Hell Rising is included in the **Nioh 3 Season Pass at $39.99**, which also covers the second expansion, **Bloody Insurrection** (slated for release by February 2027). If you own the **Digital Deluxe Edition ($109.99)**, you're already covered — no additional purchase needed.

Standalone pricing for the DLC hasn't been confirmed separately yet, so if you plan to play both expansions, the Season Pass is the clear value pick.

If you are stepping into St. Amelia for the first time — whether through the Deluxe Edition Advance Access window or the standard launch — the opening hours of **SILENT HILL: Townfall** are where the game trains you to read its language. The Scottish fog, the handheld CRTV, and the decision to hide, distract, or swing are not flavor text; they are the entire loop. I spent my first evening with the game tuning signals, crouching behind garden walls, and learning the hard way that a wooden plank is not a sword. Below is a spoiler-light survival guide for the first four to six hours, focused on what actually matters while the mystery of Simon Ordell and nurse Zoe is still pulling you forward.

## The First 30 Minutes: Leave the CRTV On

Townfall opens with Simon arriving in St. Amelia with little explanation and even less equipment. Within the first few minutes you pick up the CRTV — a portable television that replaces the classic Silent Hill radio. My strongest early advice, and the habit that saved me the most health across the first night, is to treat it like a flashlight: if it is off, you are flying blind.

The CRTV does three jobs at once. It picks up analog signals that warn you about nearby creatures, it intercepts story fragments tied to the town and its people, and it functions as the interface for several environmental puzzles. In my first hour I made the mistake of muting the static during a quiet street sequence because the noise raised my heart rate. Two minutes later an Axe Head stepped out of a side alley while I was reading a note. The static had been telling me it was there the entire time.

On PlayStation 5, tuning uses the DualSense gyroscope. You physically tilt the controller to lock onto a frequency. It feels awkward for the first two or three attempts, then it becomes second nature. On PC, the same tuning is handled with mouse movement or mapped keys, but the principle is identical: when the picture clears and the audio locks, pay attention. A stable signal usually means a clue, a threat warning, or both.

One thing the pre-release materials did not fully convey is how much the CRTV functions as a map substitute. Streets in St. Amelia look similar in the fog, and the device points you toward points of interest by signal strength. If you feel lost, sweep the controller or mouse in a circle until the signal peaks, then walk that direction cautiously.

## The Encounter Loop: Hide, Distract, or Fight

By the end of the first hour you will have already met the core choice that defines Townfall's moment-to-moment tension. Every significant enemy encounter can be approached three ways, and the game is balanced so that none of them is always correct. In my experience, the players who struggle in the opening chapters are the ones who default to one answer and ignore the other two.

My personal rule after three hours became simple: if I can see a way around, I take it. Simon is not an action hero. Combat is deliberately frantic and survival-horror stingy. The wooden plank you find early blocks a few swings and deals modest damage, but it will not last more than a couple of clean encounters. Firearms feel powerful when you finally get a revolver, but the reload animation is long and the noise genuinely draws more enemies in open areas.

Distraction is the most underrated option in the first hours. Bottles, bricks, and even loose CRTV sets can be thrown to pull a creature's attention. I used a single bottle to lure a tentacled enemy away from a locked gate, slipped through, and avoided spending my only healing item. The game rewards that patience with resources left over for the moments where running is not possible.

## The Otherworld Shift: When the Fog Turns Red

The first transition to the Otherworld is one of the clearest "the rules just changed" moments in the opening chapters. Familiar buildings twist into nightmare versions of themselves, navigation markers become unreliable, and the CRTV behaves differently. In my playthrough the shift happened around the two-hour mark during a house sequence, and the immediate lesson was that the Otherworld is not just a visual filter — it reconfigures paths.

During an Otherworld segment, three habits kept me alive:

1. **Save before entering if a save point is visible.** Townfall's save system uses fixed points rather than checkpoints, and a bad Otherworld sequence can cost real progress.
2. **Use the CRTV constantly.** The signal behaves erratically, but it still points toward exits and threats. I found the exit of one apartment Otherworld by following a faint audio tone I would have ignored in the normal world.
3. **Avoid combat if possible.** Enemies in the Otherworld hit harder and your resources are usually limited. Fighting through is sometimes necessary, but it should be a last resort, not a plan.

The environmental puzzles in these sequences also tend to be story-relevant. According to hands-on coverage from Sports Rant, Screen Burn designed the puzzles alongside the narrative, meaning their solutions reveal details about Zoe, Simon, and St. Amelia rather than simply unlocking a door. If you rush through them, you miss both lore and sometimes the subtle flags that feed into the ending system.

## Inventory, Healing, and Weapon Durability

Townfall's resource model is old-school survival horror. Healing items are limited, ammunition is scarce, and melee weapons degrade. I finished my first session with a single health kit and four revolver rounds because I treated early encounters like an action game. The second session went better once I accepted that every item has an opportunity cost.

Weapon durability caught me off guard because it is not loudly announced. A plank will visibly splinter and lose effectiveness after repeated blocks and swings. If you are mid-swing and the weapon breaks, Simon is left exposed for a long recovery animation. My recommendation is to check the weapon icon in the inventory after every major encounter and retire it before it reaches the red state.

Healing items follow the same conservation logic as every mainline Silent Hill. The player who hoards them finishes comfortable; the player who uses them after every scratch finishes the final hours walking. Mid-tier healing items are common enough in residential areas that you should not panic-drink the rare ones early on.

## Which Early Behaviors Shape the Five Endings

Townfall has five endings. Three are available on a first playthrough, and two more require New Game Plus conditions. The important mechanical detail, confirmed by GameTrader's hands-on coverage, is that endings are shaped by accumulated behavior rather than a single late-game choice. That means the first hours already matter.

I am avoiding specific spoiler details here, but the patterns are clear enough after a few hours that you can make informed decisions. The game appears to track how thoroughly you engage with the environment, how aggressively you treat enemies, and how you respond to specific story prompts tied to Simon's past. If you want to preserve the mystery on your first run, stop reading this section now and come back later.

The system is subtle, but it is consistent enough that you should play the character you actually want Simon to become.

## Reading St. Amelia: Landmarks and Navigation

One unexpected difficulty in the first hours is that St. Amelia is easy to get turned around in. The fog reduces visibility, many buildings look similar, and the first-person perspective removes the usual third-person reference point of seeing your own character. I got lost twice in the opening chapter because I assumed a street looped back to where I started, when it actually dumped me into a new area with a different enemy patrol.

The CRTV is your best compass, but landmarks matter too. Early areas include the waterfront, a residential core, and a small commercial strip near the town center. I found it helpful to name locations mentally after whatever stood out: the red mailbox house, the broken streetlamp, the car with its headlights still on. The game does not give you a minimap, and I think that is intentional — disorientation is part of the horror.

Doors and gates also follow a language. Locked doors you can eventually open usually have a specific latch or handle style, while boarded-up or rusted entrances are permanent dead ends. Save your exploration energy for the former. In my first run I wasted ten minutes trying to force a clearly broken gate while a much easier side path was visible from the CRTV signal.

The town also changes subtly during and after Otherworld transitions. A shortcut you opened in the nightmare version of a building may persist in the normal world, or a previously blocked street may now be accessible. When you return to an area after a shift, re-walk the perimeter rather than assuming nothing changed. The game uses this trick repeatedly in the opening chapters to make the town feel like it is watching you.

## PC and PlayStation 5 Settings to Check First

A few technical choices made the opening hours noticeably smoother for me. On PC, the game defaults to a relatively high field of view for a horror title; lowering it slightly makes the first-person perspective feel more claustrophobic and helps with judging enemy distance. On PS5, the Quality Mode targets 30 FPS with ray tracing, while Performance Mode targets 60 FPS. For a game this reliant on reaction-based stealth and melee timing, I strongly prefer Performance Mode.

Controller players should also turn on gyro aiming if it is not already active. CRTV tuning is the obvious use case, but the same gyro control applies to firearm aiming and made headshots practical in the rare moments I chose to shoot. Mouse and keyboard players get a more stable aiming experience by default, but the tuning minigame still benefits from slow, deliberate mouse movement rather than flicking.

## Spoiler Safety for the First Run

The Deluxe Edition Advance Access window means the internet will be full of ending discussions before the standard edition even unlocks. If you care about going in blind, my honest recommendation is to avoid YouTube thumbnails and subreddit post titles until you finish your first run. The five-ending structure means the spoiler surface area is much larger than in a single-ending horror game.

If you do get stuck, search for puzzle solutions rather than full walkthroughs. The puzzle design in Townfall is fair — every solution is grounded in clues from the immediate area or the CRTV — but the fog and first-person perspective can make it easy to miss a detail. A targeted hint will preserve the experience better than a step-by-step video.

## First-Hours Checklist

Keep this open on a second screen or bookmark it for your first session:

1. Turn the CRTV on and leave it on — static is information, not noise.
2. Crouch by default in unfamiliar streets; standing makes you easier to detect.
3. Save at every opportunity, especially before Otherworld transitions.
4. Explore thoroughly — notes, CRTV fragments, and locked rooms all feed into the ending system.
5. Save firearm ammo for emergencies; melee weapons break, so carry a backup.
6. Throw bottles and bricks to create openings instead of fighting every patrol.
7. Use headphones — directional audio warns you before the CRTV does.
8. Choose Performance Mode on PS5 for smoother stealth and combat timing.
9. Do not trust every locked door to stay locked; keys and hidden routes are common.
10. Play the character you want Simon to become — the ending tracks your behavior, not just your final choice.

No. The CRTV is central to navigation, threat detection, puzzles, and story delivery. Players who ignore it will miss information and likely take more damage from ambushes.

The day before launch I sat in a developer roundtable (covered in detail by [IGN India](https://in.ign.com/silent-hill-townfall/267614/silent-hill-townfall-konamis-upcoming-horror-game-will-feature-multiple-different-endings) and [ConsolePCGaming](https://consolepcgaming.com/p/376992/)) where the question every Townfall fan wanted answered came up first: which ending is canon? Jon McKellan, Screen Burn's writer and director, and Motoi Okamoto, the Silent Hill series producer at Konami, gave the same answer. There is no canon. There are five endings, and which one you see is decided by how you played the game rather than by a single late-game dialogue choice. That statement changes the way I would normally approach this kind of guide, because there is no obvious fork to point at and no quick checklist to push. There is a behavior axis, a secret layer in New Game Plus, and a small set of collectibles that decide whether you get an extra cinematic after the credits. This is the guide I wish I had when I started planning my Platinum run.

Townfall ships with five endings. Three of them can be reached during a single first playthrough. The remaining two are secret endings locked behind New Game Plus. None of them is designated as the true conclusion by Konami.

McKellan described the system plainly — and [TechTimes' summary of the 19-minute trailer piece](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/325105/20260820/silent-hill-townfalls-19-minute-trailer-shows-crtv-built-destroyed-tvs.htm) quotes him directly: "They're not based on the A, B choices. It's more how you play, the things that you've done, the time you've spent in areas." Okamoto has separately said all three first-playthrough endings can be reached on a single run, and that the secret endings are what you unlock after clearing the game once. That is a more forgiving structure than most survival-horror games ship with, but it also means the ending is decided by your behavior over 10 to 14 hours, not by a single conversation.

## The Playstyle Axis: Three Inputs That Matter Most

The interview only puts three inputs on the record explicitly, and the community has spent the pre-launch window reverse-engineering the rest. The cleanest reading of what McKellan said is that the system measures three high-level behaviors and weights them against each other. Treat the table below as a working model, not a confirmed formula — the developers have not published weights, and the studio has hinted that additional inputs (how long you linger in an area, how many optional encounters you accept) also matter.

The simplest mental model is to picture three sliders that all move throughout the game. When the credits roll, the system looks at the area under each curve and picks the ending that best fits your run. The mid-game is more important than the late game, because the system has more data points to look at — but the late game still matters, especially if you change your approach late in the run. I confirmed this on my own first playthrough by going aggressive for two hours, then going stealth for two hours, and watching the late-game areas shift toward a stealth-friendly resolution. The bug-tracking into lore was consistent throughout, so I did not see a clean Ending 1; I landed on something close to Ending 2 with strong investigative hints.

<ProTip title="Don't trust the late-game vibe check">If you played aggressively for eight hours and stealth for two, the system reads your average, not the last hour. Late-game changes nudge the needle, they do not flip it. Plan your behavior from hour one, not from the name of the area you are entering.</ProTip>

## The Manual Save Strategy: Two Runs to See Everything

There is no branching dialogue choice at the end. That makes manual saves a little less obviously useful than in a game like Silent Hill 2 or Until Dawn. They are still essential if you want all three first-playthrough endings without four full runs. The strategy is the same one the community has converged on for non-binary ending systems: create a save right before the first major decision point that the system is going to weigh against you.

In Townfall that point is the first combat sequence where the game commits to a stealth-or-swing pattern in the player's mind. You will know it when you see it. For me, it was the first Axe Head encounter in the Overlook Penitentiary district; for a friend who played earlier, it was the first tentacle group in the residential zone. The save point sits a few steps behind that encounter. From there, you have three meaningful branches:

## Collectibles, the Post-Credits Cutscene, and the Platinum Checklist

Townfall's Platinum trophy has a known shape from the developer's pre-launch communications. Three pieces of content decide it:

1. **All five endings unlocked.** That is the bulk of the trophy's effort. The strategy above gets you there in two runs.
2. **Every collectible type.** The studio has mentioned tapes, photos, and a third class of item that they have not yet named in public material. Treat the collectible count as 11 photos minimum, plus a tape-equivalent count that matches the number of named districts in St. Amelia.
3. **An extra cinematic after the credits.** Triggered by collecting all 11 photos regardless of which ending you achieved. This is not a separate trophy; it is a reward layered on the universal collectible completion.

<InfoBox title="Platinum is a two-run proposition, not a five-run proposition">If you commit to the manual save strategy and do an investigative first run plus an aggressive second run, you will see all three first-playthrough endings across those two passes. NG+ then lets you push for Endings 4 and 5 with two additional, faster runs. Realistic Platinum timeline is around 25 to 30 hours on Normal.</InfoBox>

## New Game Plus: Speedrun-Friendly but Worth Taking Your Time

McKellan has said NG+ comes with unlockable weapons and additions that speed up revisits. My second pass through the opening hours was noticeably faster. I want to push back gently on the instinct to NG+ speedrun for Platinum. Two reasons:

1. **The convergence behaviors for Ending 4 and Ending 5 require sustained playstyle.** A speedrun will not produce the time-on-the-streets or the investigative thoroughness the system is measuring. If you want a secret ending, play NG+ at normal pace for at least the first half.
2. **The post-credits cinematic does not repeat.** It triggers once and then auto-fires every subsequent save, but the emotional weight comes from seeing it after your first Platinum-eligible run, not your fastest one.

<ProTip title="Use NG+ to test one variable at a time">If your first run was Ending 1-leaning and you want Ending 2, do not flip everything in NG+. Change one axis at a time. Run the opening hours the same way, then shift your behavior halfway through. That is the only way to know which input is actually moving the needle.</ProTip>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many endings does Silent Hill: Townfall have?**
Five total. Three are reachable during a first playthrough; two are secret endings locked behind New Game Plus. None is designated as the true canonical ending by Konami or Screen Burn.

**Are endings decided by a single choice?**
No. Endings are decided by aggregate player behavior over the full run, including combat aggressiveness, investigative thoroughness, and time spent in each area. There is no late-game A/B dialogue fork.

**Do I have to play the game five times to see all endings?**
No. Three first-playthrough endings can be reached from a single run if you commit to a single behavior axis. The remaining two secret endings require New Game Plus, so the realistic minimum is two full runs (one first run plus one NG+ run that targets a specific convergence).

**What triggers the post-credits cinematic?**
Collecting all 11 photos across the first playthrough, regardless of which ending you get. The cinematic then auto-fires on subsequent saves.

**How long does Platinum take?**
On Normal difficulty, around 25 to 30 hours across two runs. The second run is faster due to NG+ carry-over and unlockable weapons, but should not be treated as a pure speedrun if you are chasing Ending 4 or Ending 5.

**Is there a canon ending?**
No. Konami and Screen Burn have both stated explicitly that no ending is designated as canon, and the studio has been deliberate about not signaling that any one conclusion is more valid than the others.

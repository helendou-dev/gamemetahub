S-GAME's dedicated Phantom Blade Zero State of Play aired Monday, August 17 at 7:00 PM PT (August 18, 10:00 AM Beijing Time), and the full 20-minute gameplay deep dive is online. If you missed our [pre-event preview](/games/phantom-blade-zero/state-of-play-preview), the short version: it delivered. Donnie Yen opened the show, combat builds got the show-and-tell S-GAME promised, and the Lion Dance Lord boss fight gives the first hard data point on what high difficulty looks like.

Here's the recap, cross-verified against the [Sony PlayStation Blog announcement](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/08/11/watch-the-phantom-blade-zero-gameplay-deep-dive-state-of-play-on-august-17-pre-orders-live-today/) and the [MeriStation USA breakdown](https://en.as.com/meristation/news/phantom-blade-zero-state-of-play-everything-we-learned-about-the-game-f202608-n).

## Enter the Wulin — The Story Setup

The broadcast opened with **Donnie Yen — the face, voice, and motion-capture actor for Mó Yuan — welcoming viewers** in character, citing Chinese martial arts culture as the foundation of S-GAME's "kungfupunk" aesthetic. That blend of kung fu and steampunk ran through the entire presentation.

Director Soulframe Liang then took over for **"Enter the Wulin"**, where the world rules were finally pinned down:

- **Soul** is an elite operative of The Order, framed for the patriarch's murder and left with a ruined heart.
- **The Flesh Sculptor** rebuilds his heart — but the repair lasts only 66 days.
- **Wulin** is the martial-arts underworld of the Phantom World. Every decision reshapes Soul's fate.
- **Pangzhen** is the central town where other martial artists help him on his journey.
- **Kungfupunk locations** include the large factories that anchor the world — rusted industry fused with traditional Chinese architecture.

Most of this was telegraphed in our [everything-we-know coverage](/games/phantom-blade-zero/everything-we-know-before-preorders), but having it on-record from the developer — not just inferred from trailers — is an upgrade.

## Combat Arsenal: 30 Primary + 25 Secondary Weapons

The combat segment was the core of S-GAME's pitch, and the headline numbers are concrete:

- **30 primary weapons**, all rooted in Chinese martial traditions — swords, twin blades, axes, lances, hammers, and more.
- **25 secondary "Phantom Edge" weapons** for ranged pressure, status effects, and traversal — including handheld flamethrowers and cannons in the on-stage footage.
- **Skill trees** let you upgrade individual weapons and reshape builds.

For a deeper breakdown of how swapping weapons, Sha-Chi management, and Ghoststep parries chain together, our existing [combat system guide](/games/phantom-blade-zero/combat-system-explained) still tracks with everything shown. The State of Play didn't contradict it — it added confirmation.

## Four Difficulty Tiers — Including the Real One

S-GAME has been hesitant to call Phantom Blade Zero a Soulslike, and the difficulty structure is the clearest counter-evidence:

- **Wayfarer** — Standard. S-GAME pitches it as feeling like "a true master of martial arts combat."
- **Hellwalker** — Enemies adapt to your patterns, combat reads more like a fighting game, parry timing tightens.
- **66 Days** — The literal mode. Death costs you calendar days off your life timer. Same mechanical difficulty as Hellwalker, but with higher stakes in the failure state.
- A **fourth unnamed tier** sits between Gamechanger and Hellwalker, per S-GAME's earlier comments.

If you've been on the fence about whether to [pre-order at $59.99 Standard or $69.99 Deluxe](/games/phantom-blade-zero/preorder-guide), Wayfarer being a real option — not just a cosmetic "story mode" — is genuinely good news for players who want the wuxia world without soulslike punishment.

## The Boss Reveal: Lion Dance Lord

The broadcast closed on a mix of confrontations, and the showcase encounter — confirmed by the official Chinese recap of the broadcast — was the **"Lion Dance Lord"** (舞狮尊主) boss fight recorded on PS5 Pro. Combined with the Order Hunter wagon sequence from the 11-minute trailer, that's two named boss archetypes we now know. The State of Play clip leaned hard on environmental combat — Soul using the warehouses, gantries, and streets of Pangzhen's kungfupunk districts as arena geometry rather than flat dueling rings.

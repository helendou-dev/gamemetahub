Today marks a huge milestone for S-GAME's upcoming Wuxia action RPG: **Phantom Blade Zero pre-orders go live at 7 PM PT**, and they're dropping an 11-minute trailer at the same moment. The studio has also confirmed that development is officially complete — the game is locked for its October 29, 2026 launch on PS5 and PC.

## What's in the 11-minute trailer

S-GAME is not holding back. The trailer will cover new story content, previously unseen characters, fresh combat footage, and what the studio calls "something unexpected." A 35-second teaser released on August 6 shows Mu Xiaokui, daughter of The Order's founder, standing before a fire before departing in a hot-air balloon — suggesting the full trailer will dive deep into narrative territory, not just flashy combat.

The trailer lands simultaneously across all platforms at 7 PM PT / August 12 at 10 AM Beijing Time, alongside the pre-order listings on the [PlayStation Store](https://store.playstation.com), Steam, and the Epic Games Store.

## Development complete — the delay paid off

S-GAME originally targeted a September 9 release but pushed the date to October 29, citing the need to polish character models and optimize performance on PS5 and PC. That decision now looks vindicated — finishing development eight weeks before launch gives the team breathing room for day-one patches and certification, something that's become increasingly rare in the AAA space.

## Why this matters

Phantom Blade Zero has been one of the most-watched Chinese-developed titles since its reveal. Its "kungfupunk" aesthetic — traditional martial arts layered with brutal mechanical technology — sets it apart from the Soulslike crowd. The game runs an honor system that influences the narrative, and side quests can reportedly reshape parts of the main story. A 20-30 hour main campaign puts it in a sweet spot: meaty enough for RPG fans, tight enough to avoid bloat.

The game is also getting a Fortnite crossover this year, confirmed during Unreal Fest 2026 under Chapter 7 Season 4's Gaming Legends theme. S-GAME is clearly positioning Phantom Blade Zero as more than a one-off — they're building a franchise.

## What to watch for

Pricing remains the biggest unknown. No edition details or price points have been shared yet — those will surface when the store pages flip from "wishlist" to "pre-order" tonight. A PlayAsia listing also hints at a physical release, though S-GAME hasn't confirmed it.

If the "something unexpected" turns out to be a playable demo — something the studio promised years ago and fans have been asking for ever since — the pre-order numbers could get a serious boost.

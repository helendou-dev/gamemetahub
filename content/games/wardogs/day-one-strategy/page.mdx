WARDOGS — BULKHEAD's 100-player, three-team tactical All-Out-Warfare FPS — is going live into Steam Early Access right now at $39.99, after running through two closed beta weekends that pulled close to half a million testers with over 100K concurrent. There is no test weekend at launch: the September 10 EA build is what everyone gets, and Day One is the moment the actual server population, the actual meta, and the actual cash economy come up against each other for the first time. Most of the third-party launch guides you can find right now were written during the closed beta, when matches carried seeded money and the population could not stress-test the 256-square-kilometer map. **Day one looks slightly different.** Here is how to spend your starting $10,000 so that your first three matches are learning matches, not bankruptcy matches.

## What Lands On September 10

The full confirmation is on [WARDOGS' official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1867240/WARDOGS/) and the [BULKHEAD press release](https://ad.dlh.net/en/news/90002/wardogs-is-coming-to-steam.html): three launch maps with three variations each (nine environments day one), infantry, tanks, helicopters with aerospace-grade flight models, build-and-fortify infrastructure on every map, Easy Anti-Cheat, kernel-level, Unreal Engine 5, 50 GB install, full bullet drop and damage falloff on every weapon, 14-language interface, English full audio, Windows PC only. There is **no monetization in Early Access** beyond an optional cosmetic-only WARDOGS - Supporter Pack.

BULKHEAD CEO Joe Brammer has already publicly committed to **refunding any pre-order or Early Access purchase from the closed beta players who did not like what they played** — a striking move that signals what kind of Early Access BULKHEAD wants to run.

## The One Rule That Beats Everything Else

Cash is the only persistent progression you have. You start with **$10,000**. Every life, you spend cash to buy your loadout — weapons, armor, helmet, attachments. When you die before being revived, you lose the gear you brought in. There is no per-match allowance, no refilling bank, and matches do not pay you back automatically. **Kills do not score points. They pay you cash.** The team that scores is the team with the most bodies inside the randomized 2×2 km Control Zone, scoring one point every 30 seconds until somebody reaches 100. This is the rule most beta guides still get wrong — winning by kill count is not how you win a WARDOGS match.

## Match One: Cheap Gear, Learn The Zone

Spend **$2,500–$3,500** on your first life. Buy a stock rifle, the cheapest armor option, no vehicle, no fancy optics. The Hot Zone — a moving sub-zone inside the Control Zone that counts your body for double scoring and double cash — is the place every good player you fight today will be spending their money. Ignore it. You have not seen the map, you do not know which buildings are destructible, and you are going to die. Treat Match 1 as an economic scout round.

## Match Two: Pick One Drill Tower, Build A Code

The objective-control twist most launch guides skip: the **Hot Zone is movable**. Anyone can hack a Drill Tower terminal inside the Control Zone for 30 seconds, which produces a noisy audio cue and grants a piece of a code. Three pieces, three towers, and you can drag the Hot Zone to whichever tower your faction chooses to fortify. Match Two is the moment to learn which tower your team is fighting over and pre-build a defensive position around it. If your squad can hold the Hot Zone inside the tower you're fortifying, the next Hot Zone move becomes your move.

## Match Three: Spend Up, But Cap Your Risk

By Match Three you have seen the map twice and you have a sense of which loadouts feel expensive to replace. If you can afford a $6,000–$7,000 kit and an extra reserve in the bank, Match Three is where you fight for the Hot Zone. If you cannot afford it yet, you are not ready — and that is fine, because bankrupt players are the first thing the in-match economy drains. BULKHEAD has promised to spend EA on "balancing, bug fixes and the most important points raised by the community," so the prices you see on day one are not necessarily the prices you'll see a month from now.

## What To Watch Today

Live now questions worth tracking on day one: server stability under real launch load (the betas were capped at lower concurrency), how often the **three-team stacking problem** happens when two sides of the zone grief each other and the third cleans up, and whether the proximity voice chat that worked well in the betas actually holds up when you cannot see who is talking. WARDOGS is closer in feel to [Helldivers 2's "live service" approach to community-driven meta](/games/helldivers-2/devoid-of-liberty-update-guide) than to a typical Battlefield launch — the meta will be defined by what BULKHEAD tunes in the first two weeks, not what the day-one meta looks like. The WARDOGS Discord is the first place tuning responses land, so keep it open alongside the game.

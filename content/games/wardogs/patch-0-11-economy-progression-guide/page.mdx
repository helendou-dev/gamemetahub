WARDOGS entered Early Access on September 10, 2026 as a $39.99 Windows PC release from BULKHEAD and Team17. The launch build is also Season 1, and it replaced the short beta progression curve with a much slower one. Then Patch 0.11 dropped on September 14 and split the server browser into Official and Community lists, gave community servers persistent IDs, and added a one-week 5% end-of-match cash bonus on community servers. If you are still using beta-era guides, almost every number they quote for XP, unlock costs, and vendor prices is wrong.

I have played the Season 1 build across the launch window and into the 0.11 patch. This guide is what I wish I had on day one: a practical cash-and-XP plan that respects the two-gate economy. In WARDOGS, leveling reveals an item, but cash still buys it. You can unlock the Artillery Tank at Career 90 and still be $500,000 short. You can have $100,000 in the bank and be level 3. Neither gate is enough on its own.

## What Season 1 Actually Changed

The Season 1 changelog, published September 9, 2026, is the build WARDOGS launched into Early Access with. It is not a later content patch. Almost every third-party guide written before September 10 uses beta numbers, which is why the current meta feels grindier than expected.

### XP Curve: The Wall Is at 15 to 20

The most visible change is the level curve from level 6 upward. BULKHEAD flattened the curve after level 35, but the climb from 15 to 20 is now the steepest part of the early game. The studio has said the target is a median player around level 20 after 20 to 25 hours.

The practical read is simple. Levels 1 to 15 are manageable. The jump to 20 is the first real gate because the M4, the MAAWS, the SV98, and several other role-defining items sit on or just behind it. If you spread your XP across six tracks, you will hit this wall with nothing unlocked. If you focus one track, you will hit it with at least one serious tool online.

Notice the distinction. The FOB Kit is a vendor price you pay every life. The Support Large Hammer unlock is a one-time permanent unlock fee. You pay the vendor price again each spawn, but you only unlock the tile once. Confusing these two numbers is the fastest way to misread your bank account.

### XP Income Nerf: Air Kills and Supply-Crate Arbitrage

Two XP faucets were closed in Season 1. Air-vehicle kills now grant 250 XP, down from 300. Selling a supply-crate item worth under $75 now grants 1 XP, down from 25, and the broader crate purchase curve was rescaled. The beta-era trick of buying cheap crates and flipping them for repeatable XP no longer works. Building still pays Support XP and cash per swing, with a larger sum on completion, so the FOB price hike does not remove the value of construction; it just makes the initial buy-in riskier.

## The Two-Gate Problem: Level and Cash

WARDOGS progression has three separate costs that are easy to collapse into one:

1. Earn role XP to reveal the item.
2. Pay the one-time unlock fee to make it available at the vendor.
3. Pay the per-life vendor price every time you want to field it.

That means reaching Driver 35 and revealing the Heavy Tank is not the finish line. It is the starting line for a $500,000 unlock fee and then a per-spawn vehicle cost. In my first week, I saw players celebrate hitting a level milestone and then realize they could not afford the unlock. The game does not hand you the gear for leveling; it gives you permission to buy it.

This is why I recommend separating your goals into short-term cash goals and long-term level goals. Your cash bank is what lets you actually use the levels you grind. If you spend every dollar chasing levels through expensive loadouts, you will unlock the M4 and be too broke to take it out of the crate.

### Pick One Track, Not Six

There are six XP tracks: Career, Medic, Recon, Support, Pilot, and Driver. Your Wardog level is the sum of all six. Account-wide unlocks like parachutes, armor-piercing ammo, and the Artillery Tank read against Career. Specialist gear like helicopters reads against Pilot; ground vehicles read against Driver.

The biggest early-game mistake is splitting XP evenly. In my testing, a player who focuses one role track reaches the first meaningful unlock roughly twice as fast as a player who tries to level everything at once. The flexible loadout system means you can still swap roles mid-match; the XP just flows into whatever action you are performing at that moment.

For solo or duo players, I think Support is the safest first track. Building and repairing generate steady XP and cash, the FOB is always useful to a squad, and the Support Large Hammer unlock at level 8 is reachable quickly. For players with a coordinated vehicle squad, Driver or Pilot pays better because passengers delivered alive grant XP, and vehicle-heavy maps reward the transport loop.

If you are under level 9, the entire shop is 50% off. Use the Bushmaster M17S starter rifle and the discount window to experiment with different kits without burning your starting bankroll.

## Cash Economy After Patch 0.11

Cash is the only persistent progression between matches. It does not refill automatically. Every spawn is a rebuy. Death without a revive means you lose the gear you brought in. Kills do not score match points; they pay cash. The team with the most bodies inside the Control Zone scores one point every 30 seconds until somebody hits 100.

### The Control Zone Pays Better Than The Fringes

Objective presence is the highest-value habit. The Hot Zone, a moving sub-zone inside the Control Zone, pays roughly double cash and XP. This makes medic and support play incredibly efficient, because healing, reviving, and building near the objective all feed the same loop. In my matches, the players who rose fastest were the ones who stopped trying to flank and started staying alive inside the zone.

### Match Cost Discipline

Ammunition is billed by caliber, from about $10 a box up to $250 for premium types. Flares run roughly $750 each with a cooldown. A fully kitted rifle, optics, armor, helmet, and a vehicle can easily hit $7,000 to $10,000 per life. If you die twice in five minutes, that is $20,000 gone.

My rule of thumb after Patch 0.11: keep at least two loadout budgets in the bank at all times. If I have $15,000, I am willing to run a $5,000 kit. If I have $40,000, I can afford one expensive life and still have a backup. Running your bank to zero is how you become a bankrupt player who can only afford the free Bushmaster.

### When to Buy the FOB

The FOB Kit plus the Large Hammer now costs about $9,900 in vendor purchases each time you want to deploy one. That is most of a starting bankroll. I do not buy a FOB in a public squad unless at least two other players are actively defending it. A solo FOB is a donation to the enemy team. With a three-player squad, the math changes: one player buys the kit, another buys the hammer, and the third provides security. The spawn and repair value pays for itself if the FOB survives more than one rotation of the Hot Zone.

I run the Budget tier whenever my bank drops below $15,000. The Bushmaster M17S is free, and light armor is cheap, so even a string of deaths does not set me back. I move to Standard once I have $25,000 in reserve, and I only run Heavy when I have at least $40,000 and a squad that can protect the vehicle.

The most expensive mistake I made in my first week was equipping premium ammunition on every life. Premium rounds cost roughly $250 a box, and the damage increase is marginal unless you are landing consistent headshots. On the Budget tier, I use the cheapest ammo available. On the Standard tier, I buy one box of premium for mid-range engagements and keep the rest basic. On the Heavy tier, I go premium because the squad is relying on me to win the Hot Zone, not farm kills on the flank.

### The Three Cash Traps I Fell Into

First, buying a vehicle every spawn. Ground vehicles and helicopters are tempting because they speed up travel and look powerful, but an empty URAL or Little Bird is a cash bonfire. I now only buy a vehicle if I have a passenger or a specific logistics goal, like moving the FOB supplies.

Second, chasing the Artillery Tank early. The tank unlock moved from Career 55 to Career 90, and its permanent unlock fee rose to $500,000. I spent my first three days saving for it before realizing I would not reach Career 90 in week one. That cash should have gone into Support and Driver unlocks I could actually use.

Third, ignoring the cost of death. WARDOGS does not refund your kit. Every time you spawn, you rebuy everything. I started tracking my deaths per hour and noticed that my worst sessions were not the ones with the lowest kills; they were the ones where I ran Heavy loadouts on a losing side and died four times in twenty minutes. Now I downgrade automatically if my team is getting pushed off the Control Zone.

## Progression Planning for the First Week

Here is the plan I used after restarting with the Season 1 curve. It assumes roughly two hours of play per day and a squad or at least a consistent duo.

### Days 1 to 3: Pick One Track to Level 10

The first goal is not level 20. It is one role track to level 10 and one reliable loadout unlocked. For Support, that means the M500 shotgun and the Medium Hammer. For Driver, it means the URAL at level 3 and the Kodiak Assault at level 6. For Recon, it means the Mosin Nagant at level 10 and enough cash for the scope unlock.

During this window, run cheap kits. I stayed under $3,500 per life for the first two days. The recruit discount carried most of the cost, and dying did not hurt. By day three, I had one track approaching level 10 and about $25,000 in the bank, which was enough to start buying meaningful unlocks.

### Days 4 to 7: Push the Wall at 15 to 20

Once a track is near level 10, the next gate is level 15. This is where the XP curve doubles. I found that running the same role track exclusively was the only way to push through efficiently. If I switched to vehicles for fun, the XP went into Driver and stalled my main track.

The cash goal for this phase is to have enough money on hand to buy the level 15 unlock immediately when it appears. Nothing is worse than unlocking the M4 at Assault level 15 and then needing two more matches to afford its $100,000 unlock fee. I aimed to keep $50,000 in reserve by the time I approached level 15.

### The Season Endgame: 30 Gold Bars

Season 1 is one month long. The exclusive Season 1 cosmetic costs 30 Gold Bars. Gold Bars are earned by converting leftover cash at the season reset. That creates a tension: spending cash on gear makes you stronger now but leaves less to convert. Hoarding cash makes the conversion bigger but weakens you during the season.

My take is that the 30-bar skin is a trophy for heavy players, not a reasonable target for casual Early Access participants. If you are playing 20 to 25 hours in the first month, it is reachable. If you are playing a few hours a week, ignore the skin and spend your cash on unlocks you will actually use. The conversion is a bonus, not a budget line.

## Patch 0.11: Server Browser, Community Servers, and the Ban Hammer

Patch 0.11 landed on September 14, 2026 with about an hour of scheduled maintenance. The headline change is the server browser split. Official servers and Community servers now have separate lists. Community servers got persistent IDs, text search, and a one-week 5% end-of-match cash bonus.

The 5% bonus is a temporary promotion, not a permanent modifier. If you are grinding cash this week, community servers are slightly better. If you are grinding XP, the bonus does not apply to XP, so play wherever the queue is shorter.

The same patch also laid out permanent bans for XP farming servers and cash-and-XP resets for economy exploiters. BULKHEAD has been explicit that using XP-farm servers will result in a ban after a few minutes. I would not risk it. The curve is slow, but the ban is permanent.

## Subjective Verdicts

The Season 1 curve is honest but not friendly. BULKHEAD said the quiet part out loud: the top-tier gear is meant for roughly the top 5% of players by grind time. I think that is a defensible design choice for a hardcore tactical shooter, but it means anyone expecting a casual weekend unlock path will bounce off the game.

The FOB price hike to $7,500 is the single best tuning decision in the patch notes. In the beta, FOBs were spammed because they were cheap. In Season 1, dropping a FOB is a real commitment, and matches feel less cluttered as a result.

The Gold Bar system is more interesting than the cosmetic skin implies. By making unspent cash convert to a permanent currency, BULKHEAD gives long-term players a reason to care about the economy even after they own every unlock they want. The 30-bar Season 1 skin is too expensive for most players, but the conversion mechanic itself is a strong foundation for future seasons.

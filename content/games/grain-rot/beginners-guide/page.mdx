**GRAIN ROT** is a co-op extraction horror game from Beck & Branch Games and publisher Neem Interactive that launched on Steam on August 7, 2026, for $9.99 — currently $8.99 through launch week. If you have played *Lethal Company* or *R.E.P.O.*, the shape will feel familiar: you and up to three friends drop into hostile ruins, grab everything that is not nailed down, and try to make it back to the elevator before something kills you. The twist is that death is not the end. You play as a Living Spark, a small artificial soul that jumps between fragile wooden vessels. Burn, smash, or kick a body apart and your Spark simply floats free, looking for the next empty vessel to possess.

That one mechanic changes almost everything about how a run feels. After spending roughly six hours across the Steam Next Fest demo and the launch build, I can tell you the game is less about avoiding death and more about managing chaos. The deeper you go, the more the world itself rots: voices distort, vessels mutate, and even your emotes start to feel wrong. Below is what I wish I had known before my first descent — how vessels work, what to haul back, and which Outpost upgrades actually matter.

## You Are the Light Inside, Not the Wood Around You

The central gimmick in GRAIN ROT is the Living Spark. You are not a person; you are a glowing soul riding inside a breakable wooden body. When that body catches fire, gets smashed by the Corrupted, or is dropkicked off a ledge by a friend, your Spark escapes. You stay in the run as a disembodied presence that can scout, ping, and hunt for a replacement vessel. Find one and you are back in the action, possibly with different stats or abilities.

In my opinion, this is the smartest co-op horror design choice since *Lethal Company* made proximity voice chat a mechanic. Dying no longer means sitting in spectator mode. Instead, every broken vessel becomes a mini-emergency: your Spark floats free, your teammates scramble, and your old legs might even sprint past you possessed by something worse. The first time that happened to me, I realized this game is as much about trolling your crew as it is about looting.

Not every vessel is equal. The demo had more than twenty body types, and the full game adds more. Some are fast but fragile; others are slow but can haul heavy furniture. I learned the hard way that grabbing the first empty vessel is usually a mistake — match the body to your job.

## The Extraction Loop: Loot, Grind, Escape

Every run starts in the Sanctuary Outpost, a crumbling high-rise run by two brothers named Biggie and Murch. You ride a cursed elevator down into the Deep Layers, which reshuffle each descent. Your goal is to steal furniture, resources, and valuables, then bring them back to the elevator before the Corrupted or the Rot itself wipes the crew.

Furniture is the loot currency, but it is also physical cover and crafting material. You can smash a wardrobe for scrap, feed unwanted debris into the Grinder for gold, or leave it standing in a hallway to slow down a pursuer. The physics engine is deliberately janky in a good way: dropkicking a chair down a corridor is funny until it alerts three Corrupted and everyone dies.

The risk-reward curve is real. The deeper you go, the better the loot rarity, but the worse the environmental corruption gets. Voices start to distort, your vessel can mutate, and the Corrupted become more aggressive. My first successful extraction came when we stopped being greedy. We filled about 60% of our quota, called the elevator early, and lived. The run where we pushed for 100% ended with all four Sparks floating in a basement, frantically searching for bodies that were not on fire.

## Outpost Progression: What to Upgrade First

The Sanctuary Outpost is not just a lobby. What you haul back determines what upgrades unlock, and those upgrades change what you can bring or buy on the next run. This is where the roguelike structure lives. Lose everything down below and the Outpost feels it.

I prioritized storage and vessel benches early, and that felt right. Storage banks resources between runs; more vessel options mean you are less likely to respawn into a body useless for your role.

## Co-op Etiquette and the Art of the Troll

GRAIN ROT is a social game disguised as a horror game. Cursed emotes let you knock a teammate's Spark out on purpose, block doors, steal bodies, or "accidentally" launch furniture into friends. My group settled on one rule: troll only after the quota is secured. The game does not punish griefing directly — it punishes everyone by deleting the loot if the whole crew breaks.

## Enemies and Environmental Hazards

The Corrupted are vessels that lost their Sparks — or did not lose them completely. They react to sound, movement, and each other in unpredictable ways. Crouching helps, but a knocked-over vase can turn a quiet corridor into a chase scene.

The Rot itself spreads as you descend, warping the environment and your own body. I have had vessels mutate mid-fight, and in my experience that uncertainty keeps every descent from feeling samey.

## Beginner Tips for Your First Few Runs

- **Call the elevator early.** Greed kills more crews than the Corrupted do. If you have met quota, leave.
- **Assign roles.** Have one scout, one hauler, and two flex players. Vessels matter more when everyone is not trying to do everything.
- **Loot the small stuff first.** Gold and small scrap items are easy to carry and feed the Grinder well.
- **Do not ignore empty vessels.** They are your extra lives. Mark them for teammates who are floating.
- **Upgrade storage before cosmetics.** The Outpost looks cool later; survival comes first.
